Is a PPL enough?
not private

What to notice. One question does most of the work: is anyone paying beyond an equal share of the direct costs? If so, the flight exists for someone else's purposes and it is no longer private, whether or not the pilot makes a profit. Equal means the pilot pays a share too, and only direct costs count. Classes and Parts as the lesson gives them (CASA's plain English guide on classification of operations; verify current text).

Try it: a farmer offers you $400 to photograph his paddocks, and a mining company offers a fee plus fuel to fly two geologists. What do the two have in common?

Choose each preset: the first is aerial work for reward (Part 138), the second air transport (an air operator's certificate). Both fail on the same point: someone is paying the pilot to fly, so a PPL is not enough.

Try it: your friends offer to split the whole bill so you fly for free. Nobody makes a profit. Private?

Choose Pilot flies free: no. The share is not equal, because you pay nothing, so they are paying for your seat. Split it equally including yourself and it is private again.
