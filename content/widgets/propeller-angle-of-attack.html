Helix angle
Blade angle of attack
Windmilling: the air drives the propeller

What to notice. The blade angle never moves, but the helical path swings forward as the airspeed rises, so the angle of attack $=$ blade angle $-$ helix angle shrinks. A blade at a small angle of attack absorbs little torque, so with the throttle untouched the RPM climbs: that is a fixed-pitch overspeed in the making. (Shown at the instant the speed changes, before the RPM responds; the 21° blade angle is illustrative.)

Try this: at a 21° blade angle, go from 80 kt to 120 kt. What happens to the angle of attack?

Helix angle $\tan^{-1}(80/345) = 13°$, so the angle of attack is $21° - 13° = 8°$. At 120 kt the helix angle is $\tan^{-1}(120/345) = 19°$, leaving only $21° - 19° = 2°$. The blade has gone from working hard to barely working, so it absorbs far less torque and the RPM rises.

Try this: stay at 120 kt and make the blade finer, 18°. Why does a fine-pitch propeller overspeed sooner?

$18° - 19° = -1°$: the blade is already at a negative angle of attack and the air is driving it round. A smaller blade angle means a lower angle of attack at every airspeed, so the propeller unloads, and the RPM reaches the red line, at a lower airspeed.
