What to notice. In most trainers the attitude indicator and DI run on vacuum and the turn coordinator on electrics, so one failure never takes all three gyros. A vacuum failure is the sneaky one: the gyros slow, drift and topple slowly and often without a flag, and only the suction gauge tells you early. Lose electrical power and the turn coordinator dies (and an electric attitude indicator, where fitted). The ASI, altimeter, VSI and compass need no power at all. The times here are compressed and illustrative.

Try it: in cruise the attitude indicator shows a gentle right bank, but the turn coordinator's wings are level and the compass is steady. Which instrument do you believe, and what do you check?

Press Gyros drifting: two independent instruments (the electric turn coordinator and the self-contained compass) agree that you are not turning, so the vacuum attitude indicator is the one lying. Check the suction gauge: it reads zero. Fly by the outside horizon and stay visual.

Try it: the alternator fails and the battery later runs flat. Which gyro instruments are left?

Press Electrical failure: the turn coordinator shows its flag, but the vacuum-driven attitude indicator and DI keep running, and the ball still shows balance because it needs no power. That is exactly why the turn coordinator is on a different power source from the other two.
