What to notice. In AM the carrier’s strength follows your voice while its frequency stays fixed, so the outline of the radio wave is your voice. Press two transmitters at once and AM turns the clash into a squeal everyone hears; FM would quietly drop the weaker call. The picture is not to scale: a real 118.1 MHz carrier makes tens of thousands of cycles or more for each cycle of your voice.

Try it: what does the transmitter do to your voice, and what does the receiver do?

The transmitter generates a carrier on the selected frequency and modulates it, varying its strength with your voice (AM). The receiver selects that frequency, amplifies it and demodulates it to recover the audio. The transmitter modulates, the receiver demodulates.

Try it: two pilots press the PTT together. Why is AM the safer choice?

Tick the second station: with AM the listener hears a squeal or garble and knows a call was blocked, so both can be asked to repeat. Switch to FM and one call comes through clean while the other vanishes unnoticed.
