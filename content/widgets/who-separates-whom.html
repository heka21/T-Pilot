What to notice. Read each class as three answers: do you need a clearance, who keeps you apart, what must you carry. Solid links appear only where ATC separates: in Class C they reach your aeroplane from the IFR traffic, in D and E they join only the IFR traffic, and in G there are none. Class E is controlled airspace even though VFR needs no clearance; Class G is the only non-controlled class. The picture is illustrative, not to scale.

Try it: in Class C, who keeps you apart from another VFR Cessna, and who keeps you apart from an IFR airliner?

Choose Class C. ATC separates IFR from VFR, so the airliner is ATC's job (solid link). For the other VFR aeroplane ATC only gives you traffic information (dashed link): seeing it and keeping clear is still your job.

Try it: an exam option says "VFR flights need no clearance in Class E, so Class E is non-controlled airspace." True or false?

False. Choose Class E: IFR flights get clearances and separation there, so it is controlled airspace; VFR flights need no clearance but must meet the VMC minima, squawk 1200 on a serviceable transponder and keep a listening watch on the radio. Class G is the only non-controlled class.
