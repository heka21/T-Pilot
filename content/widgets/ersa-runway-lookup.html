What to notice. A runway end is never listed on its own: find the line with both numbers (24 lives on 06/24), read the length and width in metres, the surface and the slope, then check the lighting line and the remarks for anything that names your runway. The slope reads the same for both ends but you roll the opposite way on each. Example Creek is the lesson's invented aerodrome.

Try it: what do you need to know about runway 24 before you join?

Press Runway 24: 1,200 m × 30 m sealed, lit, and 0.5% up to the east, so taking off on 24 you roll downhill from the higher eastern end, about 6 m (20 ft) over the length. The remarks give right-hand circuits for 24, so join for a right-hand circuit.

Try it: could you land on runway 30 at night?

Press Runway 30 at night: 12/30 is 800 m × 18 m gravel with no lighting listed, so it is unlit and cannot be used at night. By day it is shorter and gravel, so check the performance chart before planning to use it.
