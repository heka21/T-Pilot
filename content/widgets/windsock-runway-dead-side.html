What to notice. The windsock streams away from where the wind comes from, so you land towards the wind, from the sock's tail towards its mouth, on the runway most into wind. Build the circuit from that landing direction, every turn to the left unless ERSA publishes right-hand circuits, and the dead side is always the other side of the runway. The sock's length is only a rough guide to strength; the map's distances are illustrative and magnetic variation is ignored.

Try it: at the training aerodrome the sock streams out towards the north-east. Which runway, and where is the dead side?

A sock streaming north-east means wind from the south-west, about 225°: runway 24 is the most into wind. Left-hand circuits put the pattern on the left of 240, the south-east, so the dead side is the north-west, to the right of the runway looking along 240.

Try it: the same strip on runway 32, but suppose ERSA showed right-hand circuits. Where would you descend in an overhead join?

Right-hand turns put the circuit on the right of 320, the north-east, so the dead side moves to the south-west, on the left of the runway looking along 320. You would descend there, then cross the upwind end to join crosswind.
