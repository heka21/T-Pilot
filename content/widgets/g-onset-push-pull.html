What to notice. The aeroplane pulls the same +3.5 g every time; what changes is how much your eyes lose. The reflexes that speed the heart and tighten the vessels take several seconds, so a sudden pull greys you out where a gradual one does not, and after a pushover they point the wrong way, so the same +3.5 g goes much further: the push-pull effect. The stage bands are the lesson's approximate figures for a relaxed person; the size and timing of the reflex help and of straining are illustrative.

Try it: recovering from a spiral dive, why does the lesson say "ease out" rather than "pull out"?

Compare Gradual with Sudden: the same +3.5 g reached over ten seconds leaves your vision clear, because the reflexes keep pace. Hauled in a second, the pressure at eye level falls before they act and the view greys and narrows, just as the ground is rushing up.

Try it: a down-gust lifts you against the harness, then you pull hard. Why is that worse than the pull alone?

Choose Push-pull: during the negative g the high head pressure slows the heart and relaxes the vessels. The pull then meets defences pointing the wrong way, and the same +3.5 g that only greyed you from level flight now blacks you out. Do not follow negative g with a hard pull.
