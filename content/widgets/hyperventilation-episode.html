Breathing
CO2 in the blood
Symptoms
vicious circle

What to notice. The problem is too little carbon dioxide, not too much oxygen: the faster the breathing, the lower the CO2 and the deeper the symptoms, and the symptoms speed the breathing further. Slowing to one breath every 4 to 5 seconds (12 to 15 a minute) and talking aloud lets the CO2 build back; deep breaths make it worse. The rates, the CO2 scale and the timings are illustrative; the order of the symptoms and the cure are the lesson's.

Try it: your passenger's fingers tingle and she feels light-headed on her first flight. Why not tell her to take a few deep breaths?

Press Deep breaths: from 2½ minutes the CO2 falls faster, the hands cramp and she may pass out. Press Slow and talk: one breath every 4 to 5 seconds and a conversation bring the CO2 back to normal within a few minutes.

Try it: what happens if nobody does anything?

Press Left alone and play on: the symptoms deepen to spasm and unconsciousness, after which the brainstem restores normal breathing and the pilot comes round. That recovery is a harmless faint on the ground and possibly a fatal one in the circuit.
