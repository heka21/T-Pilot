Wind at 2,000 ft
Surface wind (veered, lighter)
Buys Ballot: low pressure

What to notice. In the Southern Hemisphere the wind blows clockwise round a low and anticlockwise round a high, along the isobars at 2,000 ft and faster where they are closer together. With your back to that wind the low is on your right. Near the ground friction slows the wind and turns it across the isobars towards the low: the surface wind is veered and lighter, about 30 degrees over land and 10 to 15 over the sea. Arrow lengths show strength only roughly.

Try it: a high sits in the Bight, south of Perth. What wind does Perth get, and where is the low?

Press High in the Bight: on the northern side of an anticlockwise high the wind is from 090, an easterly. Back to the easterly, you face west and your right hand points north: the low pressure lies to the north and the high to the south.

Try it: the 2,000 ft wind over the Wheatbelt is 320. What does the Cunderdin windsock show?

Press Cunderdin: over land the surface wind is veered about 30 degrees, to 350, and only half to two-thirds the speed. Climbing out of it you would feel the wind back and strengthen.
