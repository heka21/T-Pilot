What to notice. A cruising level has to fit a window that closes from both sides: from below it must clear the LSALT (highest terrain or obstacle plus 1,000 ft); from above it must stay 1,000 ft below the cloud (VMC above 3,000 ft AMSL or 1,000 ft above the ground: 1,000 ft vertically clear of cloud and 5,000 m visibility) and below any CTA step you cannot enter. Inside that window the table of cruising levels, by magnetic track, picks odd thousands + 500 (000 to 179) or even thousands + 500 (180 to 359) above 5,000 ft. The terrain shape and the CTA floor are illustrative: the lesson gives no Perth step figures, so set the floor yourself.

Try it: magnetic track 192, LSALT 3,400 ft, cloud base 9,000 ft. Is 7,500 ft a correct cruising level?

Press 192°M at 7,500?. No: 192 is westbound, so above 5,000 ft the level must be even thousands + 500, 6,500 or 8,500 ft. The window runs from 3,400 to 8,000 ft, so 8,500 ft is only 500 ft below the cloud. 6,500 ft is the answer (3,500 and 4,500 ft also fit below 5,000 ft, where the table does not bind).

Try it: why does a forecast cloud base of 5,000 ft cap you near 4,000 ft?

Press Cloud base 5,000. VMC needs 1,000 ft vertically clear of cloud, so the lid sits at 4,000 ft. On track 068 the window is 2,500 to 4,000 ft: 5,500 ft is in the cloud and 4,500 ft only 500 ft below it, so you plan 3,500 ft (or 2,500 ft), below 5,000 ft where the table does not bind.
