What to notice. A call goes on air only if every gate says yes, and the polite ones fail first: “thanks, mate” and “thank you, tower” stop at gate 1. A correct call can still fail on its call-sign, its frequency or the radio it comes from, while a genuine MAYDAY that you later cancel is never a false one.

Try it: why is the fuel request wrong on the CTAF and also wrong on 121.5, when both frequencies are quiet?

Both stop at gate 5. A CTAF is reserved for traffic separation, so a company message blocks the position reports that keep people apart; 121.5 MHz is the emergency frequency, and somebody may need it at any moment. A quiet frequency is still somebody else’s.

Try it: what one change turns “Northam traffic, turning base runway 23, Northam” into a correct call?

Add the type and call-sign: “Northam traffic, Cessna Alpha Bravo Charlie, turning base runway 23, Northam.” Without them, every pilot in the circuit has to guess which of the three aircraft is turning base.
