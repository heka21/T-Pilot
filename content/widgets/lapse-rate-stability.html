The parcel

What to notice. Stability is a comparison of two slopes. Where the environment line leans further left (cools faster) than the parcel's line, the lifted parcel ends up warmer and keeps rising. ELR below the SALR: stable for every parcel. ELR above the DALR: unstable even for dry air. In between, conditionally unstable: stable while the parcel is dry, unstable once it saturates.

Try this: surface 26 °C, ELR 2.5 °C per 1,000 ft, a dry parcel lifted 2,000 ft. Does it sink back? Then make it saturated.

Dry: parcel $26 - 2 \times 3 = 20$ °C, air $26 - 2 \times 2.5 = 21$ °C, so it is 1 °C colder and sinks back. Saturated: parcel $26 - 2 \times 1.5 = 23$ °C, 2 °C warmer than the air, so it keeps rising. Same air, different answer: conditionally unstable.

Try this: on a hot afternoon the ELR near the ground is 3.5 °C per 1,000 ft. What happens to a dry thermal?

It cools at 3 °C per 1,000 ft while the air around it cools at 3.5, so it gains half a degree on its surroundings every 1,000 ft and keeps rising on its own: absolutely unstable. Expect thermals, bumps and, with enough moisture, cumulus.
