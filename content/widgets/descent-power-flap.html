Thrust against drag

What to notice. The speed never changes: attitude holds the 65 kt and power sets the rate of descent. More power flattens the path, cuts the rate of descent and needs a higher nose; enough power to equal the drag gives level flight. More flap steepens the path, raises the rate of descent and lowers the nose, both because the path is steeper and because the cambered wing needs a smaller angle of attack. The lesson's 9:1 clean glide (about 6.3°) and its equations are exact here; the flap L/D (7 and 5.5), the angle-of-attack shift, the power scale and the 400 ft picture are illustrative.

Try it: a glide approach at 65 kt with the first stage of flap, and the aim point is moving down the windscreen. What do you do, and what happens?

You are too high and the power is already at idle, so lower a further stage of flap and lower the nose to hold 65 kt. Press High: more flap: the angle of descent goes from about 8.1° to 10.3°, the rate of descent from about 930 to 1,180 ft/min, and the nose drops by about 4°. The aim point moves back towards you.

Try it: on a normal approach you are getting low. Back pressure or power?

Power, with the nose raised just enough to hold 65 kt. Press Low: add power: the rate of descent falls from about 500 to 280 ft/min, the path flattens from about 4.3° to 2.4° and the nose comes up about 2°. Back pressure alone would only cost speed, and slower than the glide speed the path steepens.
