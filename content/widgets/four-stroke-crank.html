Power strokes a minute, four cylinders

What to notice. Each stroke is half a turn of the crank, so one cycle takes two turns and gives one spark. The piston is at the top at 0° and again at 360°, but at 0° the inlet valve is about to open and at 360° both stay shut for the spark: the valves must work once every two turns, which is why the camshaft is geared to half speed. Real engines open the valves a little early and close them late; the timing here is the ideal textbook one.

Try it: the piston is at the top at 0° and at 360°. Why can't the valves be driven straight off the crankshaft?

Press One turn later: the piston is in the same place as at the start, but now it is the top of the compression stroke and both valves must stay shut. A cam on the crankshaft would open the inlet valve every turn. Geared to half speed, the camshaft brings each lobe round once per cycle (it has turned 180°, not 360°).

Try it: how many power strokes a minute does a four-cylinder engine make at 2,400 RPM, and at 1,200?

Each cylinder fires once every two turns: 2,400 ÷ 2 = 1,200 power strokes a minute per cylinder, × 4 cylinders = 4,800. At 1,200 RPM, half as many: 2,400. More RPM for the same charge is more power (BAKC 3.1).
