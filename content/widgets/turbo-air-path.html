What to notice. Both methods use the same centrifugal compressor; they differ only in what spins it. The turbocharger's turbine and compressor share one shaft, so the exhaust that a normally aspirated engine throws away does the squeezing, at the price of a few seconds of lag. The gear-driven supercharger answers instantly but spends crankshaft power. The layout is schematic, not a real installation.

Try it: why is a turbocharger more efficient than a gear-driven supercharger?

Press Gear-driven and follow the drive line: it comes off the crankshaft, so part of the extra power the supercharger makes is spent turning it. Switch to the turbocharger and press At the turbine: the compressor is spun by exhaust energy that would otherwise go out of the pipe, costing only a slight rise in back pressure.

Try it: why does an intercooler give more power for the same manifold pressure?

Press No intercooler: the parcel leaves the compressor squeezed and hot, and hot air is less dense at the same pressure. Tick the intercooler and replay: outside air cools the charge, so each cylinderful holds more mass of air at the same manifold pressure, with a wider margin from detonation.
