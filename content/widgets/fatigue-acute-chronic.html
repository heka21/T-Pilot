One good night fixes it?
IMSAFE

What to notice. Three separate things make you tired: last night (acute, fixed by one good sleep), the weeks behind it (chronic, which one night does not fix) and the body clock, whose low point is about 0200 to 0600 however well you slept. The 7 to 9 hours and the circadian low are the lesson's; where a run of poor nights becomes 'weeks' is illustrative.

Try it: a pilot who has slept badly for three weeks sleeps ten hours before a flight. Is the fatigue gone?

Press 3 weeks, then 10 h: last night's bar is tall, but the readout still says chronic. It accumulated over weeks and needs extended rest and attention to whatever has been disturbing the sleep.

Try it: the 0630 departure to Busselton after five hours' sleep and a stressful week. What does IMSAFE's F say?

Press 0630 Busselton: a short night (acute fatigue) and a departure close to the circadian low, so the F is a no, on top of a stressful week for the S. Add the passenger and the booking pushing you to go, and the good decision is the one you made at home: if the cloud is below your minimum, drive to breakfast.
