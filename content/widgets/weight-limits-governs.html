Landing weight after the trip

What to notice. You may take off at the lowest of the limits, never the highest. The structural MTOW and MLW never move; the climb weight limit falls as the day heats up, the runway-limited weight depends on today's runway, wind, slope and surface, and the MLW plus the trip's fuel burn caps the take-off weight when the MLW is lower. A chart that allows more than the structure does is still capped by the structure. The chart figures here are the lessons' worked examples; real ones come from the workbook charts or your POH.

Try it: the runway allows 1,185 kg and the structure 1,150 kg, but on a hot afternoon the climb weight limit is 1,095 kg. You planned 1,120 kg. What do you do?

Press Climb limit governs: the lowest limit is the climb weight limit, 1,095 kg, so you are 25 kg too heavy, about 35 L of avgas at 0.72 kg/L. Offload fuel or a bag, or wait for cooler air, when the climb limit rises (POPA 2.2). Drag the climb limit up past 1,150 kg to see the structure take over.

Try it: ECHO's MTOW is 2,950 kg but its MLW is only 2,725 kg. You will burn 250 L on the trip. How heavy can you leave?

Press ECHO: 250 L × 0.72 = 180 kg burned. Leaving at 2,950 kg you would land at 2,770 kg, 45 kg over the MLW, so the trip limit is 2,725 + 180 = 2,905 kg (POPA 2.4). Drag the fuel burn up: a longer trip burns more and lets you leave heavier, up to the MTOW.
