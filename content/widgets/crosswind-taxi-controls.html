Why

What to notice. Drag the wind round: the column follows it while it is ahead of the wing (climb into it) and jumps to the other side and forward once it is behind (dive away from it). The surface the wind can get under is the one you raise; the surface it blows over the top of is the one you lower. A high wing is most at risk with the wind from a rear quarter. How far each control moves is drawn in proportion to the crosswind and tailwind parts of the wind (illustrative); the beam, where the rule changes over, is counted as ahead.

Try it: taxiing a high-wing aeroplane with a strong wind from the right rear quarter, where do you hold the controls?

Press Right rear quarter: column left, away from the wind, so the right (upwind) aileron goes down, and forward, so the elevator goes down. The wind from behind presses on top of both, holding the right wing and the tail down. That is the lesson's check question.

Try it: you turn from taxiing into a left front quarter wind to taxiing the opposite way. What changes?

Press Left front quarter (column left, elevator neutral), then drag the wind to 135°: after the turn it comes from the right rear quarter, so the column goes left and forward. The wind moved to the other side of the aeroplane and behind it, so you dive away from it now.
