Dilution of precision (this picture)
Furthest the fix could be

What to notice. Each range carries a small error, and the receiver's clock error makes them all wrong by the same amount, so the fix can be anywhere the bands can be slid into overlap together. Spread across the sky, that is one small spot; bunched together, the bands slide almost as one and the region becomes huge. That is a high DOP, and six bunched satellites can do far worse than five spread out. This is a flat, two-dimensional slice of the sky (three satellites fix it; the real fix needs four), and the colour thresholds are a guide.

Try it: press Six bunched, then Five spread. Which gives the better fix, and why?

Five spread. The six bunched satellites look at you from almost the same direction, so their bands cross at shallow angles and slide together with the clock error: a huge region and a high DOP. The five spread ones cross at good angles and pin the fix down. More satellites in view does not mean a better fix unless the geometry is good.

Try it: how is this like taking a fix from two landmarks?

The same geometry as position lines: two landmarks 90° apart give a small region, two almost in line give a long thin one. Satellites spread across the sky are the landmarks 90° apart.
