What to notice. The altimeter is calibrated with the ISA, so even with the correct QNH it puts you on a pressure level, and cold air squeezes those levels down: you are lower than indicated. Hot to cold, look out below. The rule of thumb is about 4% of the height above the QNH station for every 10 °C off ISA (here the station is near sea level), so the error is small near the ground and grows with height; the levels are drawn to that rule.

Try it: a winter morning 10 °C colder than ISA, indicating 4,500 ft over the Stirling Range. How much clearance do you really have over the 3,600 ft peaks?

Press Stirling Range, cold morning: 4,500 × 4% × 10/10 = 180 ft, and cold means lower, so you are at about 4,320 ft. The 900 ft you think you have is nearer 720 ft, with a correctly set, serviceable altimeter.

Try it: a summer afternoon 15 °C warmer than ISA, indicating 6,500 ft. Roughly what is your true altitude?

Press Summer afternoon: 6,500 × 4% × 15/10 = 390 ft. Warm air stretches the levels apart, so you are higher than indicated, at about 6,890 ft: on a hot day the error is in the safe direction.
