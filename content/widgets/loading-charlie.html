What to notice. Each item's index units are its kilograms times its arm divided by 100, and the CG is IU × 100 ÷ weight. Reset gives the workbook's own example from page 13: ZFW 1,014 kg with 30,060.1 IU, take-off 1,114.8 kg with 33,033.7 IU, CG about 2,963 mm. Here the fuel at 2,950 mm sits just forward of the loaded CG, so burning it moves the CG slightly aft (2,963.2 mm at take-off, 2,964.5 mm at zero fuel) and the point drops almost straight down. The utility envelope is much smaller: the same load fails it on weight alone. Arms and limits are CHARLIE's (workbook pages 13 to 15); the forward limit is read from Fig 9.

Try this: the workbook example, but with 100 kg of baggage instead of 20 kg. Legal?

Baggage 100 kg is under the 122 kg compartment limit, but the take-off weight becomes 1,194.8 kg, 79.8 kg over the 1,115 kg MTOW. Something must stay behind before the CG even matters.

Try this: pilot only (80 kg in row 1), no rear passengers, no bags, full 216 L. Inside the envelope?

ZFW 687 + 7 + 80 = 774 kg, IU 19,522 + 86.1 + 2,200 = 21,808.1, CG 2,817.6 mm. Fuel 155.52 kg adds 4,587.84 IU: TOW 929.5 kg, IU 26,395.9, CG 2,839.7 mm. Both points are inside the normal envelope (forward limit about 2,799 mm at 929.5 kg).
