What to notice. A mercy flight needs every condition at once: grave need, nothing else in time, a risk the pilot in command judges acceptable for conditions they are competent in, and ATS or CASA told. One "no" and it must not be flown; and if no rule needs breaking there is nothing to declare. Watch the sky: the declaration changes the legal position only, never the cloud. The balance's weights are illustrative; verify the mercy flight provisions in the current Part 91 and AIP ENR 1.1.

Try it: the Wiluna station hand again, but now a band of low cloud and rain lies across the route. Does declaring a mercy flight help?

Choose Wiluna, low cloud: no. The risk outweighs the benefit and a VFR pilot is not competent for IMC, so it must not be flown whatever is declared. The need is for an instrument-rated crew, or for waiting.

Try it: a farmer near Southern Cross has a suspected heart attack and you can fly him to Merredin in daylight. Mercy flight?

Choose Southern Cross: no rule needs breaking, so there is nothing to declare. It is an ordinary flight carrying a passenger, and you may use the urgency procedures for priority.
