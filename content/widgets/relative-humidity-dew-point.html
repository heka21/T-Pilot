Dew point

What to notice. Cooling the air does not change its water, so the dew point stays put while the temperature falls towards it: the spread closes, the RH climbs, and at a spread of zero the air is saturated and cloud or fog forms. The dew point measures the water; the spread measures how close the air is to saturation. The RH percentages are computed from the standard Magnus formula for the saturation curve; the lesson's working figure is the spread.

Try it: overnight the RH rises from 60% to 88%. Has water been added to the air?

No. Press Evening 20/12, then Later 14/12: the dew point stays at 12 °C, so the water is unchanged. The air cooled from 20 to 14 °C, the most it can hold fell (the glass got shorter), and the same water fills more of it. The spread closed from 8 to 2 degrees: fog or low cloud is now possible if it keeps cooling.

Try it: a METAR reads 35/05. How dry is that air, and how far must it cool to saturate?

Press METAR 35/05: a 30 degree spread, very dry air, with an RH of only about 16%. It would have to cool by 30 degrees, to 5 °C, before it saturated. Compare METAR 12/11: one degree from fog.
