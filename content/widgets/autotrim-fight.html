What to notice. With auto-trim, every pull against the servo is answered by more nose-down trim, and the attitude hardly changes, so nothing looks wrong until the disconnect hands you the whole out-of-trim force at once. Pressing A/P DISC at the first surprise leaves only a small force to hold. The sequence is the lesson's; the force levels, trim travel and pitch angles are illustrative.

Try it: the autopilot does something you did not expect. Do you hold it or disconnect it?

Disconnect, with A/P DISC, holding the controls firmly for a pitch change, then fly by hand and retrim. Holding against an auto-trim autopilot makes it trim further against you, and the bigger the trim, the bigger the upset when it lets go.

Try it: why does the attitude look normal while the trouble builds?

Press Trim runs down: your pull and the nose-down trim are cancelling each other, and the autopilot is still holding the altitude, so the aeroplane barely moves. The out-of-trim force is hidden until the disconnect, when it all arrives at once.
