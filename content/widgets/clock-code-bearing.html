Clock code
Relative bearing (from the nose)
Magnetic bearing = MH + RB

What to notice. The clock and the ADF card are fixed to the nose, so turning the aeroplane changes the clock code and the relative bearing while the object, and its magnetic bearing, stay put: heading up by 30, relative bearing down by 30. Twelve o’clock is the nose, each hour is 30 degrees, and when MH + RB passes 360 you take a full circle off.

Try it: heading 155°M, a dam lies exactly off your left wingtip. What is its magnetic bearing, and your bearing from it?

Left wingtip is 270 relative: 155 + 270 = 425, minus 360 is 065°M to the dam, and 245°M from it to you. Set the heading to 155 and the object to 065 to see the dam sit on the left wingtip, at 9 o’clock.

Try it: press ADF on 040, then turn 30 degrees right. What happens to the needle and to the bearing of the NDB?

The needle moves from 040 to 010 relative, because the nose has swung 30 degrees towards the station. The magnetic bearing stays 160°M: MH went up by exactly what RB went down.
