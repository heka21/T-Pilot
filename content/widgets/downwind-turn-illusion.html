What to notice. The aeroplane flies a perfectly ordinary circle through the air (dashed), and the whole air mass carries it east. Over the ground the track is stretched, the nose and the track part company through the crosswind part, and the groundspeed swings between 65 and 115 kt. Turning downwind it looks like a slip into the turn, turning into wind like a skid out of it, but the airspeed and the ball never change: fly the turn by the ASI and the ball, not by the rushing ground. A 30° turn at 90 kt in the lesson's 25 kt wind.

Try it: turning downwind at 500 ft the ground seems to rush past and slide towards the inside of the turn. Add some bottom rudder and raise the nose?

No. Press Apparent slip: the ball is centred and the ASI reads 90 kt, so the turn is balanced. Bottom rudder would make a real skid, and raising the nose would bleed real airspeed towards a stall speed the turn has already raised: the classic low-level stall of the inside wing.

Try it: what is the groundspeed into wind and downwind, and why does the airspeed not change?

90 − 25 = 65 kt into wind and 90 + 25 = 115 kt downwind. The aeroplane only knows the air it is flying through, and the wind moves all of that air together.
