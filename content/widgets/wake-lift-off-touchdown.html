Your point vs the heavy's

What to notice. The wake exists only while the heavy's wing carries its weight: from its rotation point on take-off to its nosewheel touchdown on landing, lying along and below its path. So you choose your own points to stay out of it: airborne before its rotation and above its climb, or down beyond its touchdown and above its approach. Drag your point along the runway. The paths are drawn much steeper than life, and if the runway does not let you do it, wait.

Try it: a heavier aircraft has just taken off. Where do you lift off?

Press Off before: you are airborne at 30% of the runway, before its rotation at 45%, and climb above its path. Press Off after: you would climb into the wake that starts at its rotation point. If you cannot get airborne before that point, wait.

Try it: you land after a heavy that touched down a third of the way along. Where do you aim?

Press Land beyond: touching down beyond its touchdown point keeps your approach above its path and your landing on runway it left clear. Land short puts your final approach below its path, in the sinking vortices.
