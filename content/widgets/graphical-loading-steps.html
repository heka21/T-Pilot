What to notice. Every station is the same move: drop straight down, then go along the scale in the direction of its arrow by weight ÷ kilograms per division (fractions allowed). Fuel changes only the weight line, so the zero fuel and take-off points lie on one vertical. The scales, divisions and envelope here are illustrative and so are the weights you set; only the 1,633 kg MTOW is ALPHA's. The method is the exam's, and in the exam you use the workbook's own chart on page 10.

Try it: two people weighing 154 kg in row 1, on a scale of 77 kg per division. How far do you move, and what if you read the 50 kg scale instead?

154 ÷ 77 = 2.0 divisions, left, the way row 1's arrow points (choose "154 kg in row 1"). Counted at 50 kg per division it would be 154 ÷ 50 = 3.08, about 3 divisions: a whole division too far forward. Choose "77 counted as 50": the dashed line ends 1.1 divisions forward of the true one, and its take-off point falls outside the forward limit although the real load is inside.

Try it: why do the zero fuel and take-off points share one vertical line?

The vertical carries the index after every load except fuel, and on ALPHA the fuel sits about at the CG, so it adds weight without moving the line. Drag the fuel slider: the take-off point slides up and down the same vertical, never sideways, while the zero fuel point stays put.
