What to notice. An NDB's medium-frequency signal bends at coastlines, reflects off the ionosphere at night and off high ground, and is drowned by lightning, so the ADF needle can point confidently at the wrong thing. Know the four effects, identify the station, and cross-check with the map. A VOR's VHF signal does not suffer them. The sizes of the errors drawn here are illustrative.

Try it: heading 120° M, needle on 040. What is the bearing to the station, and which line from it are you on?

MB = 120 + 040 = 160° M to the NDB; the reciprocal, 340° M, is the line from the station you are on. Press No error.

Try it: late afternoon, a cumulonimbus 20 nm to your left, and the needle has swung towards it. What now?

Thunderstorm effect: lightning is a strong medium-frequency source and the ADF points at it. Disregard the ADF until you are well clear and navigate by map and clock. Press Thunderstorm and watch it swing with each flash.
