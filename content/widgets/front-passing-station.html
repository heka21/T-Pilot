What to notice. The slope decides the weather. The cold front's steep 1 in 50 to 1 in 100 surface lifts the warm air fast, so its weather is a narrow band (often 50 nm or less) of Cb that passes in an hour or two; the warm front's shallow 1 in 150 surface lifts it slowly, so its layer cloud and rain spread hundreds of miles ahead. Try it: a halo round the sun on a calm morning, then the cloud lowers through the day. How high is the frontal surface above you when it is 300 nm away?

A warm front is coming. Press Warm front 300 nm away: 300 nm ÷ 150 = 2 nm, and 2 × 6,000 ft is about 12,000 ft. Expect As, then Ns with hours of continuous rain and possibly fog, a backing from north to north-west and warmer air once it has passed.
