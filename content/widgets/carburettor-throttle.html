Manifold pressure
Chilling at the butterfly
carb ice risk, BAKC 3.4

What to notice. The throttle does not pump fuel: it opens the butterfly, more air rushes through the venturi, and the stronger suction at the throat draws more fuel with it, so the amount of mixture rises while its strength stays about the same (the mixture control sets that). Opening the throttle raises the manifold pressure and, with a fixed-pitch propeller, the RPM. The bars are relative; the shapes are illustrative.

Try it: why is the air coldest at a nearly closed butterfly?

Press Low power descent: the gap past the butterfly is small, so the air squeezing through it has the biggest pressure drop, and air cools as its pressure drops. Add the fuel evaporating and, on a humid day, that is where carburettor ice forms first (BAKC 3.4).

Try it: at full throttle, does the manifold pressure ever reach the outside air pressure?

Press Full throttle: it comes close but not quite, because the air still has to get through the filter and the venturi. Climb and the outside pressure itself falls, so full throttle gives less power (BAKC 3.1, about 3% per 1,000 ft).
