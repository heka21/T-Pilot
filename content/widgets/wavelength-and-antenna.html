What to notice. Every radio wave travels at the same speed, so ten times the frequency means a tenth of the wavelength. An antenna works best at a quarter or half of the wavelength: about 60 cm for VHF COM, tens of metres for HF (a long wire or a coupler), and hopelessly long for an NDB, which is why the aircraft uses loop and sense antennas. Convert kHz to MHz before dividing into 300.

Try it: what is the wavelength of an NDB signal at 300 kHz?

Convert to megahertz first: 300 kHz = 0.3 MHz. Then 300 ÷ 0.3 = 1,000 m. A wave a kilometre long cannot be matched by a short whip, which is why low-frequency transmitters need large antennas and the aircraft receiver uses loop and sense antennas instead.

Try it: why is a VHF COM antenna a short blade, while an aircraft HF antenna is a long wire or needs a coupler?

An antenna works best at a set fraction (a quarter or half) of the wavelength. VHF wavelengths are about 2.5 m, so a quarter is about 60 cm; HF wavelengths are tens of metres, so the antenna must be long or electrically lengthened with a coupler.
