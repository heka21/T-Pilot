What to notice. A wing beyond the critical angle cannot be lifted by making it work harder. The down-going aileron raises that wing's angle of attack, so the dot on the lift curve moves further into the stalled region: less lift, more drag, a faster drop and yaw towards it, the start of a spin. Keep the ailerons neutral, stop the yaw with rudder, ease the stick forward until both dots are back below about 16°, and only then roll level. The angles and timings are illustrative.

Try it: why does the dropping wing stall more deeply even before you touch the ailerons?

Press Wing drops: as the wing moves down, the air meets it from below as well as from ahead, so its angle of attack is larger than the rising wing's. That is also what keeps a spin going.

Try it: in what order do you recover from a wing drop at the stall?

Choose the rudder option and play it: ailerons neutral with rudder to stop the yaw, stick forward to reduce the angle of attack (power to minimise the height loss), and only when both wings are flying again, level the wings with aileron.
