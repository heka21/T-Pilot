Where it breaks

What to notice. The type of hypoxia is named by the link that breaks, not by the symptoms, which look alike. Carbon monoxide breaks the blood link at any altitude (anaemic); alcohol breaks the last link, so oxygen arrives but is wasted (histotoxic) and extra oxygen does little. Small breaks in several links add together.

Try it: is carbon monoxide poisoning a kind of altitude hypoxia?

Press Heater CO: the lungs link is intact (the air pressure is fine), but the blood link is broken because carbon monoxide occupies the haemoglobin. It is anaemic hypoxia, and it happens on the ground as easily as at 10,000 ft.

Try it: on the Albany night flight, which types of hypoxia stack up?

Press Albany night: 10,500 ft breaks the lungs link (hypoxic), the cigarette and a leaking heater the blood link (anaemic), and the cold the circulation link (stagnant). Three of the four at once, each small.
