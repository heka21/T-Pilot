What to notice. The slowest safe speed of a damaged aeroplane is found by test, not read from the POH: at a safe height, in the landing configuration, slow down until full control only just holds the wings level. In the lesson's example that is 65 kt with full right aileron, the same as the normal approach speed, so you fly the approach at about 75 to 80 kt instead. The aileron model, (65 ÷ V)² of full travel, the 85 kt starting speed and the bank angles are illustrative; the 65 kt minimum, the 3,000 ft check and the 75 to 80 kt approach are the lesson's.

Try it: why not simply fly the normal 65 kt approach?

Press 65 kt: full aileron. At 65 kt all of the right aileron is already in use just to hold the wings level, so a gust, a turn onto final or the flare would have nothing left to answer with. Press Below it to see what a couple of knots less does.

Try it: how much aileron is left in hand at 75 and at 80 kt?

(65 ÷ 75)² = 0.75 and (65 ÷ 80)² = 0.66 in this model: about three quarters and two thirds of full travel used, leaving a quarter to a third for gusts and turns. That margin is why the lesson adds 10 to 15 kt.
