What to notice. Static pressure reaches the capsule at once but the case only through the calibrated leak, and the needle shows the difference between them. So the VSI shows the trend straight away but takes a few seconds (about 6 to 9) to settle on the true rate, both entering and leaving a climb, and an abrupt pitch change can flick it the wrong way first. Use it to confirm a trend, not to chase the needle. The 500 ft/min climb and the size of the flick are illustrative.

Try it: you raise the nose for a climb and a second later the VSI shows only about half the rate you expect. Do you raise the nose further?

No. Press Entering the climb: the needle is still catching up because the case pressure lags behind the capsule. Hold the attitude and give it a few seconds; chasing the needle would overshoot.

Try it: why does the VSI read zero when the static vent is blocked?

The capsule and the case are then fed from the same trapped static pressure, so nothing leaks through the calibrated leak and the two stay equal: no difference, no needle movement, whatever the aeroplane does.
