What to notice. You never need to remember every document, only to ask what kind of fact the question needs. A rule about you is in CASR Part 61, your fitness in Part 67, how you fly in Part 91 and its MOS; a place or an emergency procedure is in ERSA; anything that changed recently is a NOTAM, which beats the older ERSA entry; airspace boundaries are on the VTC, VNC or ERC, never the WAC; and the aeroplane's fitness is on its maintenance release. Watch the wording: “set out” wants the law, “a summary” wants the VFRG.

Try it: the three Pinjarra questions from the lesson, one after another. Why three different documents?

Press How far?, Runway works and 100-hourly. How far you may go is about you (CASR Part 61); works this month are a recent change (NOTAM, because ERSA would not show it yet); the 100-hourly is about the aeroplane (its maintenance release). Each kind of fact lives in its own document.

Try it: tick “Guess first”, choose “Where is the VMC rule set out?” and tap the VFRG. Why is it marked wrong?

The VFRG is guidance: an accurate plain-English summary, permitted in the exam, but not law. “Set out” asks where the rule itself is, which is the Part 91 MOS under CASR Part 91. Now try “Where can a VFR pilot find a plain summary?”: there the VFRG is right.
