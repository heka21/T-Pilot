What to notice. Danger always comes first, before the casualty: fuel, fire, a turning propeller, power lines. Send for help early, before you are busy. Then the casualty's answers pick the path: talking means treat their injuries; unconscious but breathing normally means the recovery position; not breathing normally means CPR, 30 compressions to 2 breaths at about 100 to 120 a minute, and an AED if there is one. The CPR count here runs in real time at 110 a minute.

Try it: your passenger is slumped and silent after the forced landing near Merredin. What is your very first action?

Press Danger first: before you touch them, check for danger. Fuel near a hot exhaust is a fire risk: fuel and magnetos off if you can reach them safely, and get the casualty away first if there is a fire risk. Then response, then send for help.

Try it: unresponsive, but breathing normally and no sign of spinal injury. CPR or the recovery position?

Press Breathing: the recovery position, on their side with the airway clear so vomit drains out. CPR is for a casualty who is unresponsive and not breathing normally; occasional gasps do not count as normal breathing.
