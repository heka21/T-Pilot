Flying weather

What to notice. Five different pushes, one result: the air rises, cools to its dew point and makes cloud with its base at the condensation level. The lifting only starts the air moving; stability decides the shape. Stable air gives layer cloud, smooth air and steady rain; unstable air gives heap cloud, bumps and showers. Shapes and heights are illustrative.

Try it: the same moist westerly meets the Scarp on two days. Why a flat cap one day and towering cumulus the next?

Choose Orographic and flip the stability box. The lifting is identical; on the stable day the air is colder than its surroundings once lifted and stops, spreading into a cap of stratus on the hills, while on the unstable day it keeps rising on its own into towering cumulus.

Try it: what marks the sea breeze front from the cockpit?

Press Sea breeze front: the cool sea air and the warm land air meet and the air has nowhere to go but up, so a line of cumulus forms a few miles inland, right above the wind change and its shear.
