Ground effect
Induced drag

What to notice. Close to the runway the ground gets in the way of the downwash and squashes the tip vortices, so induced drag falls with no change of speed or angle of attack. You start to notice it about a span up, and within about half a span, the last 15 ft or so for a trainer, it is significant. Landing, that means a float: less drag to slow you down. Taking off, it lets the aeroplane fly before it can climb, and the drag comes back as you climb out. The curve is a shape, not a figure from the lesson.

Try it: why does an approach 10 kt fast end in a long float?

Press Float: in the last few feet induced drag is much less than on the approach, so the extra speed bleeds away slowly and the aeroplane keeps flying along the runway until it is slow enough to settle.

Try it: you haul a heavy aeroplane off a hot strip early. What happens as you climb?

Press Early lift-off, then Climbing out of it: the aeroplane flew a few feet up on the low drag of ground effect, but climbing through half a span to a span the induced drag returns and it may sink back or refuse to accelerate. Lift off at the POH speed; if you are off early, stay low and accelerate first.
