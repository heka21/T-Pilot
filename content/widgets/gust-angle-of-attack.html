Load factor in the gust
Structure
Most the wing can make here

What to notice. A rising gust tilts the airflow so it meets the wing from slightly below: the angle of attack, and the lift, jump. The extra load is in proportion to your airspeed, so the same bump at twice the speed adds twice the g. Slow, a strong gust stalls the wing for a moment instead, and the stall caps the load. The 52 kt stall, 3.8 g limit and 5.7 g ultimate are the lesson's; VNO 125 kt and VNE 160 kt are the lesson's example ASI speeds; the wing and the gust sizes are schematic. The 19 kt gust is not a certification figure: it is sized so that the load just reaches +3.8 g at VNO, as the lesson's gust diagram draws the design gust.

Try it: the same 6 kt gust at 70 kt and at 140 kt. How much more load at the higher speed?

Press 6 kt gust at 70: about +0.5 g. Press Same gust at 140: about +1.0 g. Twice the speed, twice the extra load, even though the angle of attack jumps by less.

Try it: you are in the yellow arc when the air turns rough. What happens to the load, and what do you do?

Press Yellow arc: the gust takes the wing past +3.8 g, into the band where the structure can bend permanently. Press Slowed to VA: the same gust is back inside the limit, and a stronger one (Strong gust at VA) only stalls the wing for a moment. Slow to VB or the POH turbulence speed.
