What to notice. Every meridian runs pole to pole, so a minute of latitude is a nautical mile wherever you measure it. The meridians close in towards the pole, so a minute of longitude shrinks with the cosine of the latitude: a nautical mile at the equator, about 0.85 NM at Perth, half a mile at 60°. Measure distance on the latitude scale, never along a parallel.

Try it: Perth Airport is at 31 56.5S 115 58.0E and Kalgoorlie at 30 47S 121 28E. How far apart are they?

Difference of latitude 1° 09.5′ = 69.5 NM north. Difference of longitude 5° 30′ = 330′, but at about 31.4° S each minute is cos 31.4° ≈ 0.85 NM, so about 282 NM east. √(69.5² + 282²) ≈ 290 NM. Press Perth to Kalgoorlie to see it.

Try it: at what latitude is a minute of longitude exactly half a nautical mile?

60°, because cos 60° = 0.5. Press 60° S: the brand bar is half the length of the minute of latitude.
