What to notice. Night vision is the first casualty of altitude: it starts to fade from about 5,000 ft cabin altitude, long before you notice anything else, and may be substantially reduced by 10,000 ft. The faintest lights go first. A cigarette's carbon monoxide does the same damage before you leave the ground, and supplemental oxygen puts it back. The lesson gives only the two heights; the rate of loss and the size of the smoker's handicap here are illustrative.

Try it: you are cruising home at night at 7,500 ft and feel perfectly well. Is your vision normal?

Press 7,500: the faint farmhouse lights and the dim stars have already gone. Night vision starts to deteriorate from about 5,000 ft, well below the height at which you would notice any other effect.

Try it: what do a cigarette and 10,500 ft do together on the Albany night flight?

Press Albany night: the smoke's carbon monoxide adds to the altitude, so your night vision is worse than either alone, and the runway lights and the horizon fade. Fly lower, skip the cigarette, or use oxygen.
