What to notice. An autopilot is a loop: sensors see what the aeroplane is doing, the computer compares it with what you selected, the servo moves the control, and the sensors see the result. Altitude hold works through the elevator only, so with the power reduced it holds the height by raising the nose and lets the airspeed decay towards the stall: the autopilot holds what you ask, you manage the speed. The 48 kt stall and 55 kt warning are the lesson's; the thermal, the cruise speed, the pitch angles and the timing are illustrative.

Try it: in ALT you bring the power back for a planning check without changing mode. What happens?

Press Power back and play it: the altitude stays at 6,500 ft while the nose rises and the IAS falls, until the stall warning at 55 kt. Altitude hold moves the elevator, not the throttle, so it trades airspeed for height without telling you.
