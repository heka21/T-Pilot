What to notice. The low forms at the poleward (southern) tip of the kink and the air goes clockwise round it, so the warm air surges south ahead of the low and the cold air north behind it. As it moves east it deepens, and the faster cold front catches the warm front, starting near the low, lifting the warm sector off the ground. A place in the warm sector's path gets warm-front rain, then warm-sector low St and drizzle, then the cold front's Cb, with the wind backing at each front. The shapes and the speed of development are illustrative.

Try it: in what order does the weather arrive at a place on the warm-sector side of a wave depression?

Press Wave depression, then play on and watch your aerodrome's readout: first the warm front's lowering layer cloud and continuous rain, then the warm sector's low St, drizzle and poor visibility, then (if the cold front gets there first) the band of Cb, squalls and showers, and finally clearing, showery cold air. The wind backs at each front.

Try it: why is an occlusion a poor choice for VFR flight, even though the surface temperature hardly changes?

Press Occluded. The warm air has been lifted aloft, so two cold air masses meet at the surface (hence the small temperature change), but the occlusion still has the warm front's widespread layer cloud and rain, plus Cb embedded in it from the lifted warm sector: thunderstorms you cannot see, with low cloud, poor visibility and icing.
