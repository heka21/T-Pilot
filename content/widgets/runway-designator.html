What to notice. The runway number is the magnetic direction you land on, to the nearest 10 degrees with the last zero dropped, painted at the end you touch down on. Turn the runway round and the other number is always 18 away: 06 pairs with 24, never 26. Near a 5 the number sits on a rounding edge, which is why a slow drift of magnetic north can get a runway renumbered.

Try it: a strip is aligned 166°M. What are its designators, and which way do you point landing on each?

Press Country strip: 166 rounds to 170, runway 17, roughly south. The other end is 346°M, which rounds to 350, runway 35, roughly north. 35 − 17 = 18.

Try it: a question gives a runway aligned 164°T with 3°E variation. Which runway is it?

Runway numbers are magnetic, so apply the variation first: east is least, 164 − 3 = 161°M, which rounds to 160: runway 16, and the other end 34. Set 161°M on the slider to check.
