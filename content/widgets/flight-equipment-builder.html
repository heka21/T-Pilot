What to notice. The list follows the scenario word by word: height (radio above 5,000 ft, transponder and oxygen above 10,000 ft), airspace (radio, transponder), distance (ELT beyond 50 nm), water (life jackets, rafts) and remote area (survival equipment). The ELT's trigger is distance from the departure aerodrome, not water or remoteness. The rules are the lesson's summary: verify the current lists in the Part 91 MOS and AIP, and take the fire extinguisher seat count and the extended over-water distance from the MOS.

Try it: Jandakot to Rottnest and back, about 20 nm each way, at 1,500 ft, part of it over open water out of gliding distance of land. Which emergency items does the scenario add, and which not?

Press Rottnest. Life jackets, for the water beyond gliding distance. No ELT: 20 nm is within 50 nm of Jandakot. No survival equipment (no remote area) and no oxygen (1,500 ft). The first aid kit, and a fire extinguisher if the seat count requires one, go as on any flight, and the VHF radio is needed anyway because Jandakot is in controlled airspace. Whether it counts as extended over-water flight, for life rafts, depends on the distance in the MOS: check it.

Try it: you replan the Meekatharra flight at 11,500 ft to get above a cloud layer. Which items does that add, and does it remove any?

Press Meekatharra 9,500 ft, then drag the altitude to 11,500 ft. Two rows light up: the transponder with altitude encoding (11,500 ft is above 10,000 ft) and oxygen, once your time above 10,000 ft passes the limit. Nothing goes out: the radio, ELT, survival equipment and first aid kit all stay, so the count rises from 12 to 14.
