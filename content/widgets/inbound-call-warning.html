What to notice. The lesson's figure is 6 minutes: a call by 10 nm at 100 kt. Warning time is distance ÷ groundspeed × 60, so a faster aircraft eats the same 10 nm sooner and must call earlier (by groundspeed ÷ 10 nm for the same 6 minutes), while 10 nm is the latest point for everyone. The amber and red bands are this widget's way of showing "under 6 minutes", not a rule.

Try it: you are at 180 kt. Where must you call to give the circuit the same 6 minutes?

At 180 kt you cover 3 nm a minute, so 6 minutes is 18 nm (180 ÷ 10). A call at 10 nm gives only 10 ÷ 180 × 60 = 3.3 minutes.

Try it: you call late, at 5 nm and 100 kt. How much warning does that give?

5 ÷ 100 × 60 = 3 minutes: half the lesson's figure, and the call was not made by 10 nm.
