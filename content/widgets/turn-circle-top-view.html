What to notice. Only two things size a level, balanced turn: bank and true airspeed. More bank tilts more of the lift sideways, so the circle tightens and the heading comes round faster; more speed widens it with the square of the speed and slows the rate. Weight is not in it: a heavy and a light aeroplane at the same speed and bank fly the same circle. The circles use the formula $r = V^2 / (g \tan\theta)$; the map zooms out in steps when the circle grows, so watch the grid and the scale bar.

Try it: at 90 kt, how much tighter is a 60° turn than a 30° turn?

Press 90 kt, 30° (about 380 m), then 90 kt, 60° (about 125 m): three times tighter, because tan 60° is three times tan 30°. The heading comes round three times as fast too, about 21° a second instead of 7.

Try it: same 45° bank, 90 kt and then 120 kt. How much wider is the circle?

Press 90 kt, 45° (about 220 m), then 120 kt, 45° (about 390 m): (120 ÷ 90)² = 1.78 times as wide, and the full circle takes about 40 s instead of 30.
