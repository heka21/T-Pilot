Nosewheel / main wheels
Swing

What to notice. What matters is which wheel the aeroplane pivots on. Balanced on the nosewheel, ahead of the CG, the weight behind the pivot keeps going straight and swings the tail further out, so a small swing grows into a ground loop; pushing or braking harder only makes it worse. Ease back (or go around) and the main wheels, behind the CG, take the weight: the weight now leads the pivot and pulls the aeroplane straight, like a shopping trolley. The shares of the weight, the size of the swing and the timings are illustrative.

Try it: after a fast landing the aeroplane starts to swing with the nose held down. Push forward and brake, or ease back?

Ease back, with the throttle closed. Play Hold it down, then Ease back from the same moment: forward pressure keeps the weight on the nosewheel ahead of the CG and the swing grows into a ground loop; easing back moves the weight to the main wheels behind the CG and the swing dies away. If the speed is still high or the runway short, go around.

Try it: why does a gust that the aeroplane shrugs off on its main wheels cause a ground loop on its nosewheel?

Press Main wheels first and watch the swing: the CG is ahead of the main-wheel pivot, so as the nose swings, the weight carrying straight on pulls it back into line. On the nosewheel the CG is behind the pivot, so the same weight carrying straight on swings the tail further out: the swing feeds itself.
