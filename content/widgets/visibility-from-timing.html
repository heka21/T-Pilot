Time for 5,000 m at this speed

What to notice. Distance = groundspeed × time, and 1 nm is 1,852 m, so 5,000 m is only 2.7 nm: about 1 minute 40 seconds at 100 kt. If the feature you can recognise is that close, you are at the legal floor, not a comfortable working figure. The figure is only as good as the moment you judged the feature "recognisable".

Try it: you first recognise the York silos and pass over them 2 minutes 30 seconds later at 105 kt. Is it VMC?

Press York silos: 105 × 2.5 ÷ 60 = 4.4 nm, and 4.4 × 1,852 is about 8,100 m, comfortably above 5,000 m.

Try it: the TAF says 4000. Is that more or less than the VMC minimum?

Less. Visibility in a TAF or METAR is in metres: 4,000 m is about 2.2 nm, below 5,000 m (2.7 nm) even with no cloud at all. Set 100 kt and 80 seconds to see roughly what 4,000 m looks like.
