What to notice. The VSI reading never changes with a steady wind: the aeroplane climbs through the air, and the whole air mass moves. What changes is the ground covered in each minute, so the gradient over the ground is the rate × 60 ÷ groundspeed. A headwind steepens the path and widens the margin over the obstacle; a tailwind flattens it. 1% is about 61 ft/NM and 1° about 106 ft/NM.

Try it: 600 fpm at 80 kt clears a 300 ft obstacle 1 NM away by 150 ft. What does a 20 kt wind do?

Press 20 kt headwind: the groundspeed falls to 60 kt, the gradient rises from 450 to 600 ft/NM and the margin doubles to 300 ft. Press 20 kt tailwind: 100 kt over the ground, 360 ft/NM, and the margin shrinks to 60 ft, though the VSI still shows 600 fpm.

Try it: the ridge needs 300 ft/NM and you will climb at 70 kt groundspeed. What rate must the aeroplane make?

Press Rate needed: 300 × 70 ÷ 60 = 350 fpm just reaches the top, so you need more than that, with a margin, from the POH climb chart for the day.
