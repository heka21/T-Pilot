What to notice. Volume follows pressure inversely, $V_2 = V_1 \times P_1 / P_2$: about 1.5 times at 10,000 ft and double at 18,000 ft, where the pressure is half. Climbing, trapped gas swells (gut, teeth); descending, it shrinks and air must get back in (ears, sinuses). The pressures are the lessons' ISA values, joined smoothly between them.

Try it: a 150 mL pocket of gas in the gut at sea level. How big is it at 8,000 ft, where the pressure is about 753 hPa?

Press Gut at 8,000: 1013 ÷ 753 = 1.35, so 150 mL becomes about 202 mL, a third bigger at an ordinary cruising level.

Try it: a pocket trapped at 10,000 ft is carried down to sea level. Grow or shrink, and by how much?

Press Down from 10,000: the pressure rises from 700 to 1013 hPa and the volume falls to about 0.69, roughly two-thirds. In the middle ear that shrinking pulls the eardrum in unless air gets back up the eustachian tube.
