What to notice. In any level turn the outer wing flies a bigger circle in the same time, so it is faster and makes a little more lift. In a shallow turn that is too small to beat the aeroplane's lateral stability, so it rolls back towards level unless you hold a little aileron into the turn; in a medium turn they cancel; in a steep turn the outer wing wins and the bank keeps growing, so you hold a little aileron against the turn. The tip speeds come from the turn formula and a 10.2 m span at 90 kt; where shallow becomes steep, and how fast the bank changes, are illustrative and vary with the type.

Try it: why is the overbanking tendency stronger at 60° than at 15°?

At 90 kt and 15° the circle is about 815 m in radius, so the tips 5 m either side of the centre line differ by barely 1 kt. At 60° the radius is only about 125 m and the outer tip is about 7 kt faster than the inner one: a much bigger lift difference, now stronger than the aeroplane's stability.

Try it: halfway round a 60° steep turn the bank creeps towards 70° and the nose drops. Pull harder?

No. Press 60°, hands off: that is the overbanking tendency. Check it with aileron against the turn (press 60°, held), then restore the nose attitude. Pulling harder at a steeper bank only raises the load factor; at 75° a level turn already passes 3.8 g.
