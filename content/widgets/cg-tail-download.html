What to notice. The tail balances the nose-down tendency of a CG ahead of the lift by pushing down, and the wing has to carry that download too. Slide the CG forward: more download, more lift needed, a slightly higher stall speed, very stable, and eventually not enough elevator to flare. Slide it aft: less download and a lower stall speed, but stability drains away, and beyond the aft limit stall and spin recovery may be impossible. The limits are the lesson's worked example; the stall speeds, stability and elevator figures are an illustrative model.

Try it: an aft CG lowers the stall speed. Why is that not a reason to load aft?

Press Aft limit, then Too far aft: the stall speed drops by less than a knot, but the stability gauge collapses towards zero and recovery from a stall or spin may be impossible. The forward error is the safer one.

Try it: the lesson's four-on-board load came out at 2,154 mm. What would that aeroplane feel like?

Press Four on board, 2,154: 34 mm behind the aft limit, light and twitchy in pitch, with recovery from a stall or spin no longer assured. It must not fly loaded like that.
