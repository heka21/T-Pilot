Interceptor means
Your reply

What to notice. Each interceptor signal has one meaning and one reply. Wing rocking from ahead-left then a slow level turn: follow me (rock your wings and follow). An abrupt climbing breakaway that does not cross ahead of you: you may proceed (rock your wings). Gear down, landing lights, low over the runway: land here (follow and land if safe). Your own lights answer back: flashing landing lights over the runway, inadequate; all lights on and off at regular intervals, cannot comply; at irregular intervals, in distress. At night the navigation lights are flashed at irregular intervals as well as rocking the wings, by both aircraft (ICAO Annex 2 gives the signal as rocking and flashing). Positions and timing are illustrative; the full table is in AIP ENR 1.12.

Try it: ATS tells you to continue on track, but the interceptor is signalling "follow me". What do you do?

Follow the interceptor and ask ATS for clarification. The interceptor can see you and is acting on its instructions; ATS cannot see you.

Try it: what squawk, what frequency, the moment you realise you are intercepted?

Squawk 7700 unless ATS tells you otherwise, and call the interceptor on 121.5 MHz with your identity and the nature of your flight; notify ATS if you can.
