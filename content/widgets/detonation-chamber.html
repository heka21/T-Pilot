What to notice. Normal combustion is a burn, not a bang: flame fronts sweep the cylinder and the pressure rises smoothly. Detonation comes after the spark, when the end gas ahead of the flames gets too hot or too squeezed and explodes all at once. Each syllabus cause does that: a lean mixture at high power, high manifold pressure with low RPM, fuel of too low an octane; a hot cylinder head makes all of them worse. The remedy reverses the causes. The pressures and timings here are illustrative.

Try it: a steep, slow climb out of Northam on a 40° day, and you leaned the mixture to save fuel. What happens in the cylinder?

Press Lean, hot climb: the lean mixture burns hotter and slower and the hot head heats the end gas, so it explodes before the flames reach it. You would see rough running and the CHT climbing fast. Enrich, reduce power, lower the nose and open the cowl flaps (BAKC 3.3).

Try it: why is the rule "RPM up before manifold pressure when adding power"?

Press High MP, low RPM: a dense charge at a slow piston speed sits at high pressure long enough for the end gas to self-ignite. Raising the RPM first, and reducing manifold pressure first when taking power off, means the engine is never over-boosted (BAKC 3.3).
