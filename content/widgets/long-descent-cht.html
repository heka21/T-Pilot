What to notice. Closing the throttle for a long glide does three things at once: the cylinder heads cool fast and unevenly (shock cooling), the engine sits rich and cool (fouled plugs), and the butterfly sits nearly closed (carburettor ice). Some power on, a moderate rate and a level stage keep all three away. The curves are illustrative shapes; the POH gives your engine's limits.

Try it: why is an idle glide from 4,500 ft hard on the engine but pleasant for you?

Play the idle glide: the temperature plunges as soon as the throttle closes, because the engine makes little heat while the fast descent blows cold air over it. Parts cool at different rates, which can crack cylinders, and the long spell at idle fouls the plugs (BAKC 3.3).

Try it: what does the POH-style technique change?

Choose Some power on, descending in stages, with carburettor heat as the POH requires: the line eases down instead of plunging, the plugs stay clean and the butterfly is never nearly closed. Some POHs also suggest brief bursts of throttle during a long glide; try them to see them help, but less.
