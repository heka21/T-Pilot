What to notice. The two aeroplanes have the same airspeed; the wind alone separates them. The leg is measured over the ground, so its time comes from the groundspeed, and each one-minute tick is GS ÷ 60 nautical miles long. The visibility arrives in kilometres and the distance in nautical miles: multiply by 0.54 before you compare them. Wind here blows straight along the track; at an angle only part of it counts.

Try it: TAS 95 kt, a 15 kt headwind, a 48 NM leg. How long, and how much longer than in still air?

GS = 95 − 15 = 80 kt, so 48 ÷ 80 × 60 = 36 minutes, against 48 ÷ 95 × 60 ≈ 30 in still air: about 6 minutes, and 6 minutes of fuel, more. Press To Northam and play it.

Try it: visibility 8 km and a landmark 5 NM ahead. Can you see it yet?

8 × 0.54 = 4.3 NM, less than 5, so not yet: you will see it from about 4 NM out. Press Visibility 8 km and watch the town turn green only when the dashed circle reaches it.
