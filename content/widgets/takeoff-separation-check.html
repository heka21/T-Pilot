What to notice. Three ways the aircraft ahead can clear you: off the runway (landed and vacated), out of your path (past the upwind end or turning), or airborne far enough ahead. The distance option has conditions: 600 m only when both aircraft are under 2,000 kg, and 1,800 m only on a runway longer than 1,800 m. Landing uses the same rules, measured from your intended touchdown point. The runway lengths, lift-off and touchdown points and the light-aircraft weights here are illustrative; the 600 m, 1,800 m and 2,000 kg figures are the rule.

Try it: a 2,500 kg twin lifts off ahead of you on a 1,200 m runway. What are you waiting for?

The 600 m option is off because the twin is over 2,000 kg, and the 1,800 m option is off because the runway is not longer than 1,800 m. So you wait until it crosses the upwind end or starts its turn. Choose “2,500 kg twin ahead” and drag it past the end.

Try it: the aircraft ahead weighs 1,100 kg, but your aeroplane is 2,200 kg. Can you use the 600 m rule?

No: it needs both aircraft under 2,000 kg, not just the one ahead. Choose “You over 2,000 kg”: at 600 m ahead the verdict is still wait, until it crosses the upwind end or turns (or, on a runway longer than 1,800 m, is 1,800 m ahead).
