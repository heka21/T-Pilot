All six rules met: no ignition source left near the vapour, and an extinguisher ready if anything goes wrong. Fuel may flow.

1 Smoking in the zone: a cigarette or naked flame is an ignition source right beside the fuel vapour.

2 Engine running: the hot exhaust or an ignition spark can light the vapour.

3 Not bonded: fuel flowing through the hose builds up a static charge, and without the bonding lead it equalises as a spark at the nozzle.

4 No extinguisher at hand: a fire extinguisher must be ready before fuel flows.

5 Passengers aboard: they must be off the aircraft, or supervised under the conditions the rules allow.

6 HF transmitting: radar and HF transmissions can induce currents strong enough to spark.

What to notice. Every rule closes one way of igniting the vapour, so a single unmet rule is enough to stop the refuel: you need all six. The one people forget is static: the fuel's own flow charges the aircraft, and only the bonding lead stops that charge jumping as a spark at the nozzle.

Try it: the engine is off, nobody is smoking and the extinguisher is beside the wing. Can the refueller start?

Not yet. Choose Not bonded: with no bonding lead the flowing fuel builds a static charge that can spark at the nozzle (3). Bond the aircraft and bowser, get the passengers off (5) and stop any radar or HF transmission (6) first.

Try it: the refuel is done and everything went well. What two things before flight?

Secure the tank caps, then make a fuel drain check for water and the correct fuel, once the fuel has settled.
