What to notice. Your brain judges the approach angle by the runway's shape. A narrow or upsloping runway looks like home seen from higher up, so you feel high and fly too low; a wide or downsloping one makes you feel low and fly too high. A long runway works like a narrow one. The PAPI and planned heights do not care what the runway looks like. The model and the size of each error are illustrative; the direction of every error is the lesson's.

Try it: a strip that is narrower than your home runway and slopes uphill. High or low, and what do you do about it?

Press Narrow and up: both effects make you feel high, so by eye you fly well below the glidepath and land short. Press Fly the numbers: on the PAPI and the planned heights the path is right, although the picture still looks steep.

Try it: a wide runway at a big aerodrome. Which way does it fool you?

Press Wide: from the correct path it looks flat, like home seen from lower down. You feel low, stay high and land long.
