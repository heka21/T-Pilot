Can B tell from the date alone?

What to notice. On the AIRAC cycle a change that is ready waits for the next 28-day effective date, so between AIRAC dates nothing changes: everyone switches at the same known moment, and charts from the current cycle are current. If changes took effect the day each was ready, two pilots a week apart could be flying to different versions of the same airspace without knowing it. The four changes and their days are illustrative; only the 28-day cycle is from the lesson.

Try it: on day 50, pilot B's charts are a week old. Are they current?

Choose AIRAC, mid-cycle: yes. The last AIRAC date was day 28, B updated on day 43, and the restricted area and the runway change are waiting for day 56. Now choose Random dates, same day: the runway change took effect on day 47, so B's charts show a runway that is no longer there.

Try it: B updated yesterday, and today is an AIRAC date. Why is B a cycle behind?

Choose A new cycle starts: two changes took effect this morning, day 56, together with everyone else's. B's charts from yesterday are from the old cycle, which is why you check the effective date of your AIP and ERSA, then the NOTAMs, before each flight.
