throttle ice
Humidity
Air past the butterfly (cooled 20 to 30°)
Ice to expect

What to notice. Carburettor ice needs moisture, not frost: the venturi and the nearly closed butterfly cool the air 20 to 30°, so humid air at 20° can freeze inside the carburettor. The closer the dew point to the temperature, the worse it gets, from well below freezing to about 30°, and low power widens the danger. The temperature ranges are the lessons'; the humidity boundaries of the bands are illustrative, so read the CASA icing chart for real ones.

Try it: which is the bigger carburettor icing risk, a clear, frosty, bone-dry morning or a grey, drizzly 15° one?

Press Frosty, bone dry: even gliding, the dry air sits below the bands. Press Drizzly 15°: saturated air, serious icing at any power. It needs moisture, not cold (BAKC 3.4).

Try it: 14° with a dew point of 12 after overnight rain. What changes when you close the throttle for the descent?

Press Winter morning, 14/12: 88% humidity, serious icing even at cruise power (fuel evaporation ice). Then Same air, glide approach: 14° is inside the throttle-ice range of about −5 to +20°, and the nearly closed butterfly makes it the most dangerous kind. Apply full carburettor heat before you reduce power.
