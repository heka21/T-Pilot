VFR levels above 5,000 ft
Opposite-direction traffic at

What to notice. The compass splits at 000 and 180: tracks 000 to 179 (the eastern half) take odd thousands plus 500, tracks 180 to 359 even thousands plus 500, so traffic coming the other way is 1,000 ft above or below you. It is the magnetic track that counts, not the heading. Below 5,000 ft any altitude may be flown, subject to minimum heights (RFRC 2.3); PFRA 2.6 adds that using the hemispherical levels from 3,000 ft is good practice in Class G. Confirm the rule in the current AIP.

Try it: you plan 6,500 ft on a track of 095°M. Right or wrong?

Set 095 and 6,500: wrong half. 095 is in 000 to 179, so it takes odd thousands plus 500: 5,500, 7,500 or 9,500. At 6,500 you would be at a westbound level, facing traffic head-on.

Try it: what level goes with a track of exactly 180°M?

Press Track 180: 180 belongs to the 180 to 359 half, so even thousands plus 500: 6,500 or 8,500. 7,500 is wrong by one degree.
