What to notice. Raise the angle of attack and the suction peak over the top grows and moves forward, taking the centre of pressure with it; the CG, set by the loading, stays put. Past the critical angle (about 16° here) the flow breaks away from the rear, the suction collapses and the CP jumps rearward, so the nose drops. Lift stays at right angles to the relative airflow, not to the chord. The section, pressures and percentages are illustrative.

Try it: the exam asks which way the CP moves as the angle of attack increases. Check it.

Step from Cruise 2° to Slower 8° to Near critical 15°: the CP moves forward along the chord each time (here from about 42% to 29%). Then press Stalled 18°: it jumps back to about 42%, well behind the CG.

Try it: why does the nose tend to drop at the stall?

Press Near critical, then Stalled: the gap between the CP and the CG (the arm of the nose-down couple) suddenly grows as the CP runs aft, and the lift itself falls. Both pitch the nose down.
