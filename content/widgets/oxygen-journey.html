Oxygen in the air

What to notice. Only two things push the gases: breathing moves air in and out, and the heart moves blood round; everything that crosses a wall does it by diffusion, from higher pressure to lower. The right side of the heart sends blood to the lungs, the left side to the body. At altitude the oxygen is still 21 percent of the air, but its pressure, and so the push across the alveolar wall, is smaller. The timings are illustrative.

Try it: what makes the oxygen cross into the blood, and why does altitude weaken it?

Press Diffusion, then At 18,000 ft: the oxygen diffuses because its pressure in the alveolar air is higher than in the blood arriving from the body. At 18,000 ft that pressure is about half (about 105 hPa), so the push is weaker and the haemoglobin leaves the lungs less than fully loaded.

Try it: which side of the heart pumps blood to the lungs?

Press CO₂ back: the veins bring the oxygen-poor blood to the right side, which pumps it through the lungs. The left side pumps the oxygen-rich blood from the lungs out to the body.
