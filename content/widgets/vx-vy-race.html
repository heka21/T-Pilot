What to notice. The first aeroplane is higher at every moment, so it has the better rate of climb (feet per minute, Vy). The second is higher at every distance, so it has the better angle (feet per nautical mile, Vx). A hill close to the runway is a distance problem: only the steeper climber clears it. The numbers are the lesson's example, in still air.

Try it: a 600 ft hill 1.2 NM from the runway. Which aeroplane clears it?

Play the race: the first gets there after 48 s at only 560 ft (467 ft/NM × 1.2), 40 ft too low. The second takes 72 s but arrives at 780 ft (650 ft/NM × 1.2) and clears by 180 ft.

Try it: move the hill to 2.5 NM. Does the faster climber still lose?

Press Distant hill: at 2.5 NM the first is at about 1,170 ft, well clear, and it got there sooner. Far from the runway, the problem becomes time, which is why you lower the nose to Vy once the close obstacles are behind you.
