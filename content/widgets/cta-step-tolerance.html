What to notice. You are never at a single point: navigation has a tolerance, the altimeter may be up to 100 ft out and every hectopascal of subscale error adds about 30 ft. Plan so the whole box stays in Class G: 500 ft below each step's lower limit; flying in towards a lower step, descend before you are within your tolerance of its boundary, and flying out, climb only once you are clearly past it. The along-track distances are illustrative, and the source note gives the lateral buffer only as a nautical mile or two; the tolerance for each means of navigation is in AIP ENR 1.1 (verify there).

Try it: why not cruise at 3,500 ft under the C LL 3500 step?

Choose At 3,500: the lower limit is the bottom of Class C, so at 3,500 ft you are already at the boundary, and the 100 ft of altimeter error puts you inside it. At 3,000 ft the box stays clear with 400 ft to spare.

Try it: at 3,000 ft you are approaching the step 1 boundary. When may you descend to 2,000 ft?

Choose Near the boundary: 0.6 nm short, with a 1 nm tolerance, you could already be under step 1, where Class C starts at 2,500 ft, so 3,000 ft could be inside it. Descend to 2,000 ft before you get within your tolerance of the boundary, then cross it.
