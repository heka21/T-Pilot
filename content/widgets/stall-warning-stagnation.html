What to notice. Both devices watch the stagnation point, not the airspeed: as the angle of attack rises it moves down and back under the leading edge. The reed is sounded by the suction once the stagnation point has moved below the opening, and needs no electricity; the vane is flipped up and closes a switch, so it needs the master on. On an iced wing the critical angle falls below the warning's setting and the wing can stall first. The angles here (warning at 12°, stall at 16°, iced 11°) and the stagnation point's travel are illustrative; the lesson gives 15 to 18° for the critical angle and a warning about 5 to 10 kt above the stall.

Try it: after a complete electrical failure, which type still warns you?

Press Vane, master off: the vane lifts but nothing sounds. Switch to the reed: the suction over the leading edge draws air through the horn with no electricity at all.

Try it: why can an iced wing stall before the warning sounds?

Press Iced wing: ice lowers the critical angle to below the angle the warning was set for, so at 11.5° the wing has stalled while the stagnation point has not yet reached the opening. Remove all ice and frost before flight.
