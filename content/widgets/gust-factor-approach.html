What to notice. The gust factor is the gap between the gust and the mean wind, and the usual rule adds half of it to the normal approach speed, so that the extra speed can be lost in a lull without bringing you near the stall; your POH or your instructor may set a limit on how much to add. A gust group 10 kt or more above the mean is also a turbulence warning in its own right. The shape of the wind trace is illustrative.

Try it: the TAF for your arrival gives 22012G30KT and your normal approach speed is 62 kt. What do you fly, and what else does the wind tell you?

Gust factor 30 − 12 = 18 kt; half is 9 kt; 62 + 9 = 71 kt. A gust 18 kt above the mean is well past the 10 kt that signals turbulence: expect a rough approach and strong mechanical turbulence near the ground, check the crosswind against your limit, and decide before you leave whether this is a day to be landing there at all.

Try it: at Northam the wind sock is swinging between 15 and 25 kt. How much do you add?

The sock is showing a mean of about 15 kt and gusts to 25 kt: a gust factor of 10 kt, so add half, 5 kt, to your normal approach speed.
