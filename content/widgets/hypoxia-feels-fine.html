What to notice. The two lines move apart: the worse you are doing, the better you feel. Euphoria, over-confidence and the loss of self-criticism hide the slips from the one person who could fix them, and there is no breathlessness to warn you. So the rules and supplemental oxygen decide, not how you feel. The slips are the lesson's; how fast they come at each level is illustrative.

Try it: an hour at 11,500 ft and you feel great. What does the evidence say?

Press 11,500, an hour in: the fuel log stopped long ago, a radio call slid by and the heading has wandered ten degrees. The pilot who has hypoxia is very often the last to recognise it; check objective things like the fuel log, not your feelings.

Try it: what does supplemental oxygen change?

Press On oxygen: the oxygen pressure in the lungs is restored, so the lines stay together. The Part 91 MOS oxygen requirement for unpressurised aircraft starts above 10,000 ft; check the current text for the exact conditions.
