What to notice. The pattern names the fault: which needles move, which way, and which stay still. Oil pressure down with temperature up is losing oil; temperatures up first, then pressure, is trapped heat; electrics only is the alternator or regulator; one needle alone (fuel pressure, suction, RPM) points straight at its system. The coloured arcs, the speed of each trend and the needle positions are illustrative (your POH gives the real limits); the only figure from the lessons is the voltmeter's about 14 volts while charging, sinking towards the battery's 12. The panel is a generic one: a gravity-fed trainer such as the Cessna 152 has no fuel pressure gauge, booster pump or cowl flaps. Each fault runs its course with nothing done, so you see where it leads.

Try it: oil pressure falling. Is it the engine or the gauge?

Compare Losing oil with Gauge fault?. When the engine is losing oil, the oil temperature climbs as the pressure falls, because less oil is carrying the same heat. With the gauge at fault the temperature stays on its mark. You cannot prove which in the air, so in both cases reduce power, enrich and land as soon as possible.

Try it: the ammeter swings one way or the other. Which fault is which?

Alternator: the ammeter shows a discharge and the voltage sags from about 14 towards battery voltage. Shed load to essentials and land within perhaps 30 to 60 minutes, before the battery is flat. Regulator: a very high charge and a high voltage. Switch the alternator off as the POH directs, before it damages the battery and avionics. In both cases the engine runs on, because the magnetos need no battery.
