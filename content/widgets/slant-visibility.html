What to notice. The METAR's visibility is horizontal, measured at the surface below or in the bottom of the layer. Looking down from above, your line of sight slants through the haze trapped at the top of the inversion, so far more particles lie along it, and into the sun they glow. Slant visibility can be much worse than the reported figure. The haze profile and contrast are illustrative.

Try it: the METAR shows 9999, yet descending through smoke you can barely see the runway. Why?

Press METAR 9999, descending: the surface observer looks horizontally through the thinner air under the layer, while your sight line slants through the dense band at its top. Tick Into the sun and it gets worse again.

Try it: why can you see the ground below you but not the runway ahead?

Press Under the layer: no dense haze lies between you and the runway any more, so you see it about as well as the observer reported. Now press Looking straight down: from above, the ground right beneath you shows through, because you cross the layer by the shortest path. It is the shallow look ahead that loses it.
