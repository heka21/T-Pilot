Resultant against the weight
steady glide

What to notice. Start the second arrow at the tip of the first and the resultant runs from the first tail to the last tip. Only forces pointing the same way add as plain numbers; opposite forces subtract, and two at right angles make the long side of a right-angled triangle. In a steady glide, lift and drag added this way point straight up, and the weight drawn on from their tip comes straight back to the start: no net force.

Try it: two forces of 300 N, one forward and one back. What is the resultant?

Press 300 N each, opposite: the second arrow returns to where the first began, so the resultant is zero and the body keeps whatever motion it had. Adding the sizes, 600 N, is the mistake.

Try it: in a glide at a lift-to-drag ratio of 10, lift is about 9,760 N and drag about 976 N. What do they add to?

Press Lift + drag in a glide: they are at right angles, so the resultant is √(9,760² + 976²) ≈ 9,809 N, pointing straight up, equal and opposite to the 9,810 N weight of a 1,000 kg aeroplane.
