What to notice. Every call follows the same order, location traffic, type, call-sign, position, level, intentions, location, so each one drops an aircraft onto your mental map: by the time you join, the radio alone has told you the runway, who is ahead and whom to follow. Distances are compressed, and the call-signs other than Alpha Bravo Charlie and the timings are made up for the example; markers fade because each one shows where that aircraft was when it called.

Try it: on the half-heard call, how do you know it matters to you?

You missed "Northam traffic" and the type, but the call ends with "Northam". The aerodrome name at the end is there for exactly this: the frequency is shared, and the last word tells a late listener which aerodrome the call is about.

Try it: at your inbound call, how long before you reach the circuit, and who might you meet on the way in?

10 nm at 100 kt is 6 minutes (10 ÷ 100 × 60). The aircraft from the east estimates the circuit at 22, ahead of you, and the Warrior about to depart to the south-west will be climbing out near the upwind end as you approach the crosswind join.
