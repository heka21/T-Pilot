Ailerons
Elevator and rudder
Air over the tail

What to notice. Every control works by the air flowing over it, so its power goes with the square of the airspeed: half the speed, a quarter of the force. Power throws the slipstream back over the tail, so the elevator and rudder come alive the moment you open the throttle, even when slow; the ailerons, out on the wings, get nothing from it. The V² rule is the lesson's; how much the slipstream adds, and the soft and sloppy thresholds, are illustrative.

Try it: gliding at 65 kt you add a burst of power. What happens to the rudder and to the ailerons?

Press Glide, 65 kt, then Burst of power: the elevator and rudder jump from about 42% to about 60% at once, because the slipstream speeds up the air over the tail. The ailerons stay at 42%: the aeroplane's airspeed has not changed yet.

Try it: why does the rudder work early on the take-off roll?

Press Take-off roll: at 30 kt the ailerons have under a tenth of their cruise effect, but full power puts the slipstream over the fin, so the rudder already has about a third. That is why you can keep straight with rudder long before the ailerons do much.
