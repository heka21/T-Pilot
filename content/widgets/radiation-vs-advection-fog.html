What clears it

What to notice. What makes the fog decides what clears it. Radiation fog is made by the ground cooling under a clear night sky, so the morning sun, heating that ground, burns it off. Advection fog is made by moist air flowing over a cold sea, so sunshine does almost nothing and it stays, day or night, until the wind changes direction. Timings here are illustrative; the exam wants the causes and the cures.

Try it: fog that "persisted through the day despite sunshine" with a 12 kt wind. Which kind?

Advection fog. Press Sea fog in sunshine: the sun is high and the fog has not moved, because the cold sea keeps chilling the air from below. A 12 kt wind would have mixed radiation fog away.

Try it: you plan a 0800 departure from Northam after a clear, still winter night. When do you go?

Play the night: the fog forms near dawn, thickens just after sunrise and burns off from the edges through the morning. Plan for late morning, once the sun has heated the valley floor.
