What to notice. Climbing makes the mixture richer (thinner air, same fuel), so at 6,500 ft full rich costs RPM. Lean slowly: the RPM rises to a peak at best power, slightly rich of the chemically correct 1:15; a fraction further it falls and the engine roughens. Come back to the peak and a little richer. EGT climbs as you lean and peaks near the correct mixture: rich runs cool, lean runs hot. The RPM figures are the lessons' worked example; the curve shapes between them are illustrative.

Try it: at 6,500 ft, full rich shows 2350 RPM. Lean by RPM, as RBKA 2.1 describes.

Press Full rich, 6,500 ft, then drag the knob slowly right: the RPM rises to 2400 (Peak RPM), then drops to 2370 and the engine roughens (Past the peak). Enrich back to 2400 and a touch richer (Just rich of peak). The fuel flow is now noticeably lower than at full rich, and the engine runs smoothly.

Try it: you descend from 6,500 to 3,500 ft without touching the mixture. Why is the engine rough?

Press Descended, not enriched: the denser air meets the same fuel, so the mixture you leaned for 6,500 ft is now too lean, past the peak on the lean side. Drag the knob left (enrich) until the RPM is back up and the engine smooth, or go full rich if you are joining the circuit.
