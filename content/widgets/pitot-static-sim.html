What is going on

What to notice. Every blockage traps a pressure. Pitot pressure goes only to the ASI, so a pitot fault touches nothing else; static goes to all three, so a static fault freezes the altimeter, zeroes the VSI and makes the ASI wrong whenever you leave the height where it blocked. The dashed green needles show the truth. The model is the standard atmosphere; the cabin offset for the alternate static is illustrative.

Try it: in a cruise climb the ASI creeps up although the attitude has not changed, and the altimeter and VSI look normal. What has failed?

Only the ASI is wrong, so it is a pitot fault, and a rising ASI in a climb means the pitot and its drain are both blocked: press Pitot + drain, climb. Fly attitude and power, switch on pitot heat, and do not pull up to "slow it down".

Try it: with the static vent blocked you descend from 4,500 ft. What do the three instruments show?

Press Static, descent: the altimeter stays at 4,500 ft, the VSI at zero, and the ASI over-reads, so you are slower than it shows. Select the alternate static and all three come back, reading slightly high.
