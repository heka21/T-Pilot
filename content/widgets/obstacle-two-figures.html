Ground at the base
Clearance above the top
Rule-of-thumb cruise over it

What to notice. The big unbracketed figure is the elevation of the top above mean sea level, the same datum your altimeter uses on QNH, so it is the one you clear. The bracketed figure is the structure's height above its own ground: it tells you how tall it looks, not how high to fly. Never add the two: the elevation already includes the structure.

Try it: a mast near your track reads 2,180 (520). What is the ground elevation, and what QNH altitude puts you 500 ft above its top?

Ground 2,180 − 520 = 1,660 ft AMSL. The top is at 2,180 ft, so 500 ft above it is 2,680 ft on QNH. Press 2,180 (520).

Try it: an obstacle reads 940 (380) and you cruise at 1,200 ft on the area QNH. By how much do you clear it?

1,200 − 940 = 260 ft. The 380 is irrelevant to the altimeter; comparing 1,200 with it would make you think you had 820 ft to spare.
