What to notice. Two heights, two datums. Over a populous area (a city, town or other populous area, and over a public gathering) you may not fly below 1,000 ft above the highest obstacle within a 600 m radius of the aircraft: the datum is the obstacle, not the ground, so a 100 m tower 500 m to your side raises your floor by 328 ft. Anywhere else the floor is 500 ft above the highest feature or obstacle within a 300 m radius of the point directly below you; over flat open country that is 500 ft above the surface, and a hill or a mast raises it. The red line is that floor along the track. The exceptions are taking off and landing, CASA-approved operations, a forced landing and avoiding weather or terrain, and you must always be able to glide clear of a populous area if the engine fails. The town and its obstacles are made up; the figures are the lesson's.

Try this: move to 900 m from the tower, still over the town, at 1,200 ft. Legal?

No. The tower is within the 600 m radius, so the floor is 1,000 ft above its 100 m (328 ft) top: 1,328 ft. At 1,200 ft you are 128 ft below the minimum although you are 1,200 ft above the ground beneath you.

Try this: at 600 ft over the open country, slide towards the 60 m hill. When does the floor rise, and to what?

When the hill comes within 300 m of the point below you, the floor becomes 500 ft above its 60 m (197 ft) top: 697 ft. Out in the open the floor is 500 ft above the 15 m trees, 549 ft, so 600 ft is enough there but not over the hill.
