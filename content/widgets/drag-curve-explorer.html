Best L/D (best glide) speed
Glide from 3,000 ft, still air

What to notice. Total drag is least where parasite and induced drag are equal: that speed gives the best L/D and the flattest glide. Slower or faster both cost distance, and slower also puts you on the back of the curve, closer to the stall. A lighter aeroplane has the same best L/D at a lower speed. With power shown, endurance is the bottom of the curve and range is where a line from the origin (or from the headwind speed) touches it.

Try it: after an engine failure you raise the nose from 70 kt to 56 kt to "stretch the glide". What happens?

Press Nose up: the L/D falls from 9 to about 8.2, so from 3,000 ft you reach about 4.0 NM instead of 4.4, and you are much closer to the stall. Raising the nose never stretches a glide.

Try it: why does the best range speed go up into a headwind?

Press Range into wind: the line now starts at 25 kt on the speed axis, so it touches the power curve at a higher speed. Flying faster spends less time being pushed back by the wind.
