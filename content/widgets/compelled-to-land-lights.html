What to notice. Landing or navigation lights switched on and off repeatedly mean the aircraft is in difficulty and compelled to land, often with a radio failure, so everyone else makes way: stay off the runway, and in the circuit extend or leave the pattern. Context decides the meaning: a single flash in reply to a tower’s green light at night is only an acknowledgement. Positions, speeds and timings here are illustrative.

Try it: you are on downwind and see an aircraft on final switching its landing lights on and off. What do you do?

Give way: it is in difficulty and compelled to land. Extend your downwind (or leave the pattern) rather than turning base in front of it, and expect it to land. Choose “Circuit traffic extends” to see the base turn you do not make.

Try it: at night an aircraft on final flashes its landing light once, just after the tower shows it a green. Should you give it the runway as an emergency?

No. One flash in reply to a tower’s light signal is the night acknowledgement; the steady green has already cleared it to land. Repeated, irregular switching on approach with no other explanation is the request for the runway.
