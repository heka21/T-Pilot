What to notice. Weight × arm = moment; add the moments, divide by the total weight and you have the CG. The weight check and the CG check are separate: Full fuel is 12 kg under MTOW but 34 mm aft of the limit. The tanks at 2,300 mm are aft of the CG, so burning fuel moves the point down the dashed line and forward; the take-off point and the point after the burn must both be inside. This trainer's limits are 2,000 and 2,120 mm at every weight, so its envelope is a plain box; on many real envelopes, such as the workbook's CHARLIE, the forward limit moves aft at higher weights. The aeroplane and its figures are the lesson's worked example, not your aeroplane: always use the POH.

Try this: solo, 85 kg pilot, 120 L of fuel, but the 30 kg bag strapped into the front seat instead of the locker (front seats 115 kg, baggage 0). Where is the CG at take-off and after 60 L?

The weight is unchanged at 881.4 kg, but the bag's moment falls from 30 × 3,500 = 105,000 to 30 × 2,000 = 60,000, so the total is 1,867,720 − 45,000 = 1,822,720 kg mm and the CG 2,068 mm: 52 mm inside the aft limit instead of 1 mm. After 60 L (43.2 kg, 99,360 kg mm): 838.2 kg, 1,723,360 kg mm, CG 2,056 mm. That is the "much more comfortable margin".

Try this: press Four adults: over MTOW (160 kg in front, 150 kg in the rear, 10 kg of bags, full tanks). Does taking out fuel fix it?

It is 1,108 kg, 58 kg over MTOW, and the CG is 2,195 mm, 75 mm aft. Taking out 58 kg of fuel is 58 ÷ 0.72 = 80.6, so 81 L, leaving 69 L: 1,049.7 kg, legal on weight, but the CG is 2,189 mm, still well aft, because the fuel was not the load causing it. Leave both rear passengers behind instead: 958 kg at 2,085 mm with full tanks, inside both limits. Even one 75 kg rear passenger with full fuel and no bag gives 2,131 mm, aft.
