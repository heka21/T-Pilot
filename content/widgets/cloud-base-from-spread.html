Cumulus base (above the ground)
The parcel

What to notice. The rising air cools 3 °C per 1,000 ft but its dew point falls only 0.5, so the spread closes 2.5 °C per 1,000 ft: every degree of spread is about 400 ft of cloud base. The drier the air, the higher the base; a spread of 2 or 3 degrees means a low base and possible fog. It is a rule of thumb for cumulus from surface thermals only, and the answer is above the ground at that station.

Try it: the METAR says 24/16. Lift the parcel to 2,000 ft. How far is it from making cloud?

At 2,000 ft it has cooled 6 degrees to 18 °C and its dew point has fallen 1 degree to 15 °C: a spread of 3 °C still to close, at 2.5 per 1,000 ft, which is another 1,200 ft. The base is at 8 × 400 = 3,200 ft.

Try it: Kalgoorlie reports 36/06. Why are its storms often dry?

Press Kalgoorlie 36/06: a 30 degree spread puts the base near 12,000 ft above the ground. Rain falling from that high base evaporates in the dry air beneath (virga), chilling it into strong, dusty downdraughts.
