What to notice. Take the flap away and four things change together: the nose sits higher (the clean wing needs more angle of attack), the path is flatter at the same power (less drag), the threshold speed rises with the stall speed (about 1.3 × it), and the landing roll grows with the square of that speed, with a longer float in ground effect on top. The rules are the lesson's; the example aeroplane is illustrative: stall 55 kt clean, 52 first stage, 50 full flap (so 65 kt with full flap), lift-to-drag ratios of 9, 7 and 5.5 at the same power, and the float. Use your POH's speeds.

Try it: the normal approach is 65 kt with full flap. Flapless, with a 55 kt clean stall, how much longer is the landing roll?

Flapless approach speed 1.3 × 55 ≈ 72 kt. The ratio 71.5 ÷ 65 = 1.1, and energy goes with the square: 1.1² = 1.21, so about 21% more landing roll, before the extra float. Press Flapless and compare the bars.

Try it: why is the nose higher flapless, even though the aeroplane is flying faster?

At 1.3 × its own stall speed each configuration needs the same fraction of its maximum lift coefficient, but the clean wing's curve sits lower, so it reaches that lift at a higher angle of attack, and the flatter path tilts the nose up further. Press Flapless: about 7° angle of attack on a 6° path puts the nose about level; full flap is about 5° on a 10° path, nose down about 6°.
