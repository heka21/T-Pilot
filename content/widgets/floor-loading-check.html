Floor loading
Smallest legal area (weight ÷ 450)
Against the 450 kg/m² limit

What to notice. Floor loading is weight per square metre, not weight: the same 30 kg on four times the area is a quarter of the loading, 1,000 kg/m² on the battery's base and 250 kg/m² on the board. A dense item can sit well inside a compartment's weight limit (122 kg in CHARLIE, 54 kg in BRAVO, 55 kg and 155 kg for ECHO) and still punch through the floor panel. As in the lesson, the kg/m² figure here leaves out the board's own weight, but the board goes on the load sheet. A board smaller than the base adds nothing on that side, so each side of the contact is the larger of the two.

Try it: what is the smallest square board that makes the 40 kg survey box legal?

It needs 40 ÷ 450 = 0.089 m², and the side of a square of that area is √0.089 = 0.298 m, so 0.30 m. Set the board to 0.30 × 0.30 m: 0.09 m², 444 kg/m², within the limit but only just. At 0.29 × 0.29 m (0.0841 m²) it is 476 kg/m², over. A larger board gives a sensible margin.

Try it: why does doubling both sides of a board quarter the loading?

Area is length × width, so twice the length and twice the width is 2 × 2 = 4 times the area, and the same weight over four times the area is a quarter of the loading. The battery's base is 0.20 × 0.15 m (1,000 kg/m²); the 0.40 × 0.30 m board doubles both sides (250 kg/m²); double again to 0.80 × 0.60 m and it falls to about 63 kg/m².
