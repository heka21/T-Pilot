What to notice. Radiation fog needs all four: a clear sky, a light wind of about 2 to 5 kt, moist air with a small spread at sunset, and a long night. The ground chills the air touching it, an inversion forms with the coldest air at the bottom, and the cold air pools in the valley. Calm gives dew or frost, a wind much over 10 kt or a cloudy sky gives no fog. The same inversion leaves the surface calm under a stronger wind aloft: shear at its top around dawn. Temperatures and timings are illustrative.

Try it: Northam reads 09/07 at dusk, the sky is clearing and the wind is light. Will your 0700 departure go?

Press Fog at dawn: the 2 degree spread closes in the early hours and the valley fills with fog by dawn, thickening just after sunrise. Drag on to see it lift into stratus and clear by mid-morning: plan to leave then.

Try it: same humid evening, but a 20 kt wind all night. Fog?

Press 20 kt: the wind mixes the chilled air with warmer air above, so the ground never chills a layer to its dew point and there is no inversion. No fog; at most low stratus, because the air is so moist.
