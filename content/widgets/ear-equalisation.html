What to notice. The tube is a one-way valve: air pushes out by itself on the climb, but on the descent it has to be let back in, by you, a little at a time. A cold that swells the tube shut turns every descent into an ear block. The pressures are the ISA values from the lessons; how big a difference pops the tube or hurts is illustrative.

Try it: with a head cold, is the climb or the descent the problem?

Tick Head cold and play the flight: the climb still vents, a bigger puff at a time. On the descent the swollen tube never opens, the trace dives into "pushed in" and the ear blocks. Ears and sinuses hurt on the descent.

Try it: your passenger is asleep over the wheatbelt. Why wake them before the descent?

Press Asleep: nobody swallows, so the tube stays shut and the difference builds all the way down. Awake and swallowing (Descent: swallow), the trace stays near the line.
