What to notice. The same small error in each position line moves the fix only a little when the lines cross near 90°, and a long way along them when they cross at 20°: the region of uncertainty stretches to about four times its length at 90°. Three lines form a cocked hat; take its centre. The quickest fix of all is one unmistakable point feature. The band widths and the colour thresholds are a guide, not figures from the lesson.

Try it: a railway runs 5° off your track for 30 nm. What can it tell you, and what can it not?

Lying almost along the track, it is an excellent track-error line: you see how far off you are and whether it is growing. It cannot give a groundspeed or a fix on its own: crossed with the track at 5°, any small error slides the crossing point a long way along it. Set 10° to see how long that region gets.

Try it: for a groundspeed check, why choose a road that crosses the track at right angles?

The moment you cross it is sharp and does not move if you are a little left or right of track. Drag line 2 towards 20°: the same sideways error now slides the crossing point a long way along the track, and the groundspeed with it.
