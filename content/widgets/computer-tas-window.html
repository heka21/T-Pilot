What to notice. The computer's airspeed window sets pressure height against temperature, which fixes the air density, and the disc then turns by exactly the ratio between CAS and TAS, so every CAS on the inner scale sits under its TAS. Thinner air, a higher TAS. The 2 percent rule is a quick check that overestimates a little at these heights; in the exam, use the method the question names.

Try it: pressure height 8,000 ft, OAT +5 °C, CAS 100 kt. TAS by the rule and by the computer?

ISA at 8,000 ft is −1 °C, so ISA +6; density height 8,000 + 720 ≈ 8,700 ft; 8.7 × 2% ≈ 17% → about 117 kt. The computer, working the density out exactly, gives about 114 kt. Press 8,000 ft, +5 °C.

Try it: 6,000 ft, +15 °C, CAS 105 kt?

ISA +12, density height 7,440 ft, 14.9% → about 121 kt by the rule; about 117 kt on the computer. Press 6,000 ft, +15 °C.
