Australian unit for this job
The conversion

What to notice. Each job has its own unit: feet for anything vertical, metres for runways, nautical miles and knots for navigation and speed, metres and kilometres for visibility, hectopascals for pressure and litres for fuel (with kilograms for weight and degrees Celsius for temperature). The conversions you need in the air are rough ones: 1,000 ft is about 300 m, a kilometre is a little over half a nautical mile, a hectopascal is a millibar.

Try it: the forecast says visibility 3,500 m. How far is that in nautical miles?

3,500 m is 3.5 km, and 3.5 × 0.54 = 1.9 NM. Press Visibility 3,500 m: a town on track will appear less than 2 NM ahead, about a minute at 120 kt.

Try it: ERSA lists a runway as 1,200 m. Is that long in feet?

Press Runway 1,200 m: about 3,900 ft. Read the length as feet by mistake and you would think the strip was barely a third of its real length.
