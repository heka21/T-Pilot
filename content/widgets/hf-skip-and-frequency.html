What to notice. The skip zone lies between where the ground wave fades and where the first sky wave comes down, so a nearer aircraft can hear nothing while a far one hears clearly. After dark the ionosphere weakens and only lower frequencies come back: by day use higher HF frequencies; at night lower ones. The heights, distances and frequencies here come from an illustrative model; the rules are the lesson’s.

Try it: an aircraft 150 km from an HF station hears nothing, while one 30 km away and one 1,500 km away both hear it clearly. Why?

Press The skip zone: the aircraft at 150 km is in the skip zone, beyond the range of the fading ground wave but short of where the first sky wave returns. The near aircraft gets the ground wave, the far one the sky wave.

Try it: on the ferry flight to Kalgoorlie your afternoon HF frequency fades away after dark. What do you do?

Press Same frequency after dark, then Lower at night: the weakened ionosphere now bends back only lower frequencies, so you change to a lower one.
