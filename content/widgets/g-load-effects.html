What to notice. Under positive g the 30 cm column of blood from heart to eyes weighs n times as much and blood pools in the legs, so the supply to the head fails. Vision goes first, in a fixed order: grey-out, tunnel vision, black-out while still conscious, then G-LOC. Grey-out is the warning to ease off. Negative g drives blood to the head instead: red-out, and it is painful by about −2 to −3 g. The g values are the lesson's approximate figures for a relaxed, unprotected person; how fast the picture fades within each band is illustrative, and a sudden onset is worse than a slow one.

Try it: what do you feel in a level 70° turn, and how does it compare with the aeroplane's limit?

Press 70° turn: n = 1 ÷ cos 70° ≈ 2.9 g, so an 80 kg pilot feels about 234 kg and is in the band where heaviness and the first greying begin. Press +3.8 g: the normal category limit already sits in the grey-out and tunnel-vision band, so your eyes warn you before the airframe's limit.

Try it: why is black-out not the same as G-LOC?

Press Black-out, then G-LOC: at black-out you cannot see but you are still conscious and can relax the pull; vision returns within seconds. Beyond it the brain's own supply fails and you are incapacitated for 15 to 30 seconds or more.
