What to notice. The engine answers to density height, not to the altimeter: a normally aspirated engine loses about 3% of its sea-level power per 1,000 ft from the ground up (the lessons' rule of thumb; the POH has the real figures). Each power setting has its own full throttle height, where the throttle reaches the stop: the lower the power, the higher it is, and a hot day brings it lower on the altimeter. A turbocharger holds rated power to its critical altitude (typically 12,000 to 20,000 ft) and then loses it the same way; the wing still feels the density height.

Try it: on a standard day, where is the full throttle height for 75% power? And for 65%?

Press 75%, standard day: 100 − 75 = 25% to lose, 25 ÷ 3 ≈ 8,300 ft. Drag the power to 65%: 35 ÷ 3 ≈ 11,700 ft, higher, because less power needs less air. Then press 65%, ISA +15: the 15° adds 120 × 15 = 1,800 ft of density height, so the throttle hits the stop at about 9,900 ft on the altimeter (PAKC 2.1).

Try it: you plan a 75% cruise at 8,500 ft to Kalgoorlie, 15° above ISA. Will you get it?

Press To Kalgoorlie at 8,500: the full throttle height for 75% is about 6,500 ft pressure height today, so at 8,500 ft (10,300 ft density height) full throttle gives only about 69%. Plan with the POH row for the power you will get. Switch to Turbocharged: it holds 75% there with throttle to spare.
