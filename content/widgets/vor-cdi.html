What to notice. The needle and the flag depend only on where you are and the course you have set, never on your heading: turn the aeroplane and nothing on the indicator moves. The flag reads TO on one side of the line through the station at right angles to the course and FROM on the other. Fly the reciprocal of the course you have set and the needle works backwards. The needle shows degrees off the course, so the same deflection is fewer miles close in; full scale here is 10°, 2° a dot, as on a typical indicator. The DME measures the slant from you to the station.

Try it: with 030 set the needle is centred and the flag reads FROM. Where are you, and what do you set to track to the station?

On the 030 radial, north-north-east of the station: press 030 FROM. Set the reciprocal, 210, and the needle centres with TO; fly about 210°M, corrected for drift.

Try it: you cross directly over a DME at 6,000 ft. What does it read, and what does it read 20 NM out?

Overhead it reads the height, 6,000 ÷ 6,076 ≈ 1.0 NM, although the ground distance is nil. Twenty miles out it reads √(20² + 1²) ≈ 20.02 NM: the slant matters only close in and high.
