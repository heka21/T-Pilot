Where the point sits

What to notice. Up from the temperature to your pressure altitude line, interpolating between lines, then straight across to read the density altitude. The lines slope at the 120 ft per degree of the mathematics, so a chart answer must agree with the arithmetic; where a line crosses the dotted ISA line the two altitudes are equal. Drag the crosshair or use the sliders. This chart is drawn from the lesson's formula; a POH chart may lay its lines out differently.

Try it: pressure altitude 3,000 ft, OAT 30 degrees. Read the density altitude, then check it.

Up from 30 to the 3,000 ft line and across: 5,520 ft. Check: ISA at 3,000 ft is 9 degrees, so the deviation is +21 and 3,000 + 120 × 21 = 5,520 ft.

Try it: your pressure altitude is 2,500 ft, halfway between two lines. What do you do?

Interpolate: go up from the temperature to a point halfway between the 2,000 and 3,000 ft lines, then across. At 30 degrees that gives 4,900 ft, the same as the arithmetic: 2,500 + 120 × 20.
