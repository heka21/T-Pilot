What to notice. The sea breeze is the land's doing: no heating, no Doctor. The stronger the gradient easterly, the later it arrives, and above about 20 kt it never makes it ashore. Timings here are illustrative; the exam wants the order of events and the wind change at the front.

Try it: you plan a 1300 landing at Jandakot on a typical summer day. What wind do you brief for?

Set the typical day and drag to 1300: the front is still crossing the coastal plain, so Jandakot may be on the old easterly runway or right under the front. Brief for a wind change to the south-west, shear in the circuit and a possible runway change.

Try it: why is a strong-easterly day the hottest?

Choose Strong easterly and play the day: the cool sea air is held offshore all afternoon, so nothing replaces the air baking over the land.
