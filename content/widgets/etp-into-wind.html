Groundspeed on / home
ETP from departure
Check: time from the ETP

What to notice. The ETP is where the two time lines cross, and wind tilts them: the line for the downwind trip is flatter, so the crossing slides towards where the wind comes from. There is no fuel control here because the ETP has no fuel in it. Put the groundspeed home on top, $D \times GS_\text{home} \div (GS_\text{on} + GS_\text{home})$, and check that the two times from the ETP agree.

Try it: on the 20 kt headwind leg, trouble strikes at 100 nm with only 80 nm to go. Which way is quicker?

Back: 100 ÷ 130 × 60 = 46 min, against 80 ÷ 90 × 60 = 53 min on. You are short of the ETP at 106.4 nm, so turning back is quicker even though the destination is closer.

Try it: drag the wind from a 15 kt tailwind to a 15 kt headwind on the 240 nm leg. Where does the ETP go?

From 105 nm (15 nm short of the 120 nm midpoint) to 135 nm (15 nm beyond it): the same distance either side of halfway, always on the side the wind comes from.
