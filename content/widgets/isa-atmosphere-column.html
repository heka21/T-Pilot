What to notice. The ISA temperature falls 2 °C per 1,000 ft (1.98 exactly) from +15 °C to −56.5 °C at the tropopause, 36,090 ft, then stays at −56.5 °C to about 65,600 ft. Deviation is OAT minus ISA, keeping the sign. The classic trap: 3 °C per 1,000 ft is the dry adiabatic lapse rate of a rising parcel, not the ISA lapse rate. Pressure halves by about 18,000 ft, so the 30 ft per hPa rule only works near the ground. The pressures come from the standard ISA formula, rounded.

Try it: an exam option uses 3 °C per 1,000 ft. What ISA temperature would that give at 8,500 ft, and what is the right answer?

15 − 8.5 × 3 = −10.5 °C, which is wrong: 3 °C per 1,000 ft is the dry adiabatic lapse rate. The ISA uses about 2: 15 − 17 = −2 °C (press ISA at 8,500 ft). On the Kalgoorlie day the forecast +12 °C is then ISA +14.

Try it: what does the 30 ft rule give at 18,000 ft, and why is it wrong?

1013 − 18,000 ÷ 30 = 413 hPa, but the true ISA pressure is about 500 hPa (press Half the air). Air is compressible: higher up it is thinner, so each hectopascal takes more than 30 ft. At 1,200 ft the rule gives 973 hPa, close to the truth.
