What to notice. Each layer can be managed or mismanaged, so TEM gives you three chances, not one: anticipate the threat, trap the error, recover from the undesired aircraft state. Take away one defence and the next one still saves the flight; take away all three and the chain runs to an incident. Recovering is the last chance, and it always starts by restoring a margin.

Try it: in the QNH example the pre-take-off check missed the wrong setting. Did TEM fail?

Press QNH, cruise check: no. The error escaped the first trap but the cruise check caught it before it put you 300 ft lower than you thought over the Scarp. TEM expects some errors to get past the first defence; that is why it has layers.

Try it: "low and slow on short final": threat, error or undesired aircraft state?

An undesired aircraft state: it describes the aeroplane's condition. Press Wagin, go-around: the gusty crosswind was the threat, the missing gust margin was the error, and low and slow is what they produced. The go-around restores the margin.
