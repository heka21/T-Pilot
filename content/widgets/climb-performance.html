Absolute / service ceiling

What to notice. The aeroplane climbs on what is left over: the gap between what the engine and propeller give and what level flight needs. The biggest gap in thrust comes at a low speed, Vx, the best angle; multiply by speed and the biggest gap in power comes faster, Vy, the best rate. Weight, density altitude and flap all shrink the gap, so both rate and angle fall. As the air thins Vx creeps up and Vy comes down until they meet at the absolute ceiling, where no climb is left at any speed. An illustrative trainer: at maximum weight at sea level Vx is 60 kt and Vy 75 kt as in the lesson; your POH's figures govern.

Try it: why is Vx always slower than Vy?

Press Vy, sea level and switch on thrust and drag: the biggest gap is at 60 kt. Switch back to power: multiplying by the speed moves the biggest gap to 75 kt. On the rate curve, Vy is the top, and the dashed line from the origin touches the curve further left, at Vx.

Try it: what happens to Vx and Vy as you climb towards the ceiling?

Drag the density altitude up from 0: the best rate shrinks (about 450 fpm at a 3,500 ft density altitude), Vx rises a little from 60 kt and Vy falls from 75 kt. At the absolute ceiling they meet at about 62 kt and the best rate is zero; the service ceiling, 100 fpm, comes a few thousand feet earlier.
