What to notice. Forecast winds are in degrees true; ATIS and tower winds, like runway numbers, are magnetic. Convert a forecast wind once (variation west, magnetic best; variation east, magnetic least), never an ATIS wind, then compare the angle to each runway end: the smallest angle with a headwind is the best aligned. That is not automatically the runway you choose: length, surface and the POH's demonstrated crosswind decide. Example Creek is the lesson's invented aerodrome; the components are $V\cos\theta$ and $V\sin\theta$.

Try it: the forecast changes to 200 degrees true at 20 kt. Which runway end is into wind, and what are its components?

Press Forecast 200/20. 200°T + 1°W = 201°M. Runway 24 (240°M) is 39° off: crosswind 20 × sin 39° = 12.6 kt from the left, headwind 20 × cos 39° = 15.5 kt. Runway 12 (120°M) is 81° off: 19.8 kt across and only 3.1 kt of headwind. So runway 24, with about 13 kt across it to check against the POH demonstrated crosswind, and a right-hand circuit.

Try it: from the 280/15 forecast, set the variation to 10° W and leave the source on forecast. How much does the angle to runway 24 change?

280°T + 10°W = 290°M, so runway 24 is 290 − 240 = 50° off instead of 41°: 9 degrees more, exactly the extra variation. Its crosswind rises from 9.8 to 15 × sin 50° = 11.5 kt and the headwind falls to 15 × cos 50° = 9.6 kt, while runway 30 comes to 10° off (2.6 kt across). Forgetting to convert would leave you at 40°, 10 degrees out: with a large variation the error no longer hides.
