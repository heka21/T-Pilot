What to notice. Two steps, two corrections: pressure height $= \text{elevation} + (1013 - \text{QNH}) \times 30$, then density height $= \text{pressure height} + 120 \times (\text{OAT} - \text{ISA})$. Low QNH and heat both push density height up, and heat is the big one: every degree above ISA adds 120 ft. The ISA deviation is rounded to the nearest half degree, as in the worked examples.

Try this: elevation 1,300 ft, QNH 1004, 33 °C. What is the density height?

Pressure height = 1,300 + 9 × 30 = 1,570 ft. ISA there = 15 − 3.1 ≈ 12 °C, so the day is ISA +21. Density height = 1,570 + 120 × 21 = 4,090 ft: the aeroplane performs as if it were at about 4,100 ft.

Try this: keep 1,300 ft and QNH 1004 but make it a 12 °C morning. What changes?

Pressure height is still 1,570 ft, but 12 °C is ISA, so density height ≈ pressure height, about 1,570 ft. The same aerodrome is 2,500 ft "lower" in the cool of the morning than in the afternoon heat.
