Jandakot to Northam, 48 NM

What to notice. Each step corrects one thing, always in the order I, C, T, G: the instrument and its position, then density, then wind. The density step is the big one at height and on a hot day, and it always makes TAS more than CAS above sea level. The TAS here is the 2 percent rule of thumb; the flight computer gives a few knots less.

Try it: at 6,000 ft the ASI shows 100 kt, the POH table says CAS is 2 kt less, and you have a 20 kt tailwind. Roughly what is your groundspeed?

CAS 98 kt; on a standard day 6,000 ft adds 12 percent, so TAS is about 110 kt; add the tailwind for about 130 kt. Press Whole chain to check.

Try it: same pressure height, same CAS. Is your TAS higher at +20 °C or at ISA?

At +20 °C. Press Warm day, 6,000 ft: 17 degrees above ISA adds 2,040 ft of density height, so 103 kt CAS becomes about 120 kt TAS instead of about 115.
