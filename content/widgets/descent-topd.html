What to notice. Two divisions place the top of descent: height to lose ÷ rate of descent gives the time, and time × groundspeed gives the distance. A gentler descent or a faster groundspeed starts you further out; the fuel follows the time, planned at cruise flow to be conservative. The first preset is the lesson's worked example (its 30 L/h is an illustrative figure); the other two apply the same method.

Try it: cruising at 6,500 ft, joining at 1,500 ft, 500 ft/min at 110 kt. Where do you start down?

5,000 ft ÷ 500 = 10 min; 10 ÷ 60 × 110 = 18.3 nm. Start down about 18 nm before the joining point, and plan 5 L at 30 L/h.

Try it: a tailwind gives you 130 kt on the way down. Same time, so why start further out?

Press Tailwind: the descent still takes 10 minutes, but in 10 minutes at 130 kt you cover 21.7 nm, so the top of descent moves about 3 nm further from the joining point.
