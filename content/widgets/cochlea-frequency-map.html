Pitch
Sensed in the cochlea
Do you hear it?

What to notice. Every pitch has its own place along the cochlea, high pitches near the entrance. Noise kills the hair cells there first, around 4,000 Hz, and they never grow back, so you lose the quiet, high consonants while the loud, low vowels still come through: you hear that someone spoke but not what they said. The width of each damaged band is illustrative.

Try it: after years of flying without a good headset, which part of a radio call goes first?

Press Noise damage: the hair cells around 4,000 Hz are gone, where the consonants that separate "five" from "nine" live. Press A vowel: the low vowels are still heard, so the call sounds loud enough but the words blur.

Try it: why is an older pilot with noise damage worse off on the radio than either loss alone explains?

Press Older pilot: ageing (presbycusis) takes the highest pitches as well, so the damaged band joins up with it and more of the consonant range is lost.
