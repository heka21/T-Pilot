What to notice. With the throttle fixed the elevator cannot add energy, only move it: the dashed total-energy line never moves, and the zoom swaps the speed share of the bar for the height share. Because kinetic energy goes with speed squared, the top 40 kt of a fast aeroplane hold more height than the bottom 40 kt, and twice the speed is four times the energy. Drag is ignored, as in the lesson's examples, so a real zoom gains a little less.

Try it: the same 40 kt loss from 100 kt and from 120 kt. Which buys more height?

Press 100 → 60 kt: about 280 ft. Press 120 → 80 kt: about 350 ft. The energy goes with the square of the speed, so the faster zoom has more to give for the same drop in speed.

Try it: how much more kinetic energy is there at 120 kt than at 60 kt?

Press 120 → 60 kt and look at the bar: the speed share at the top is a quarter of what it was at the start (× 0.25), because (60 ÷ 120)² = 1/4. Double the speed, four times the energy.
