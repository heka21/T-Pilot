At the aerodrome 15 nm away

What to notice. Take away any one of moisture, instability or a trigger and there is no storm. With all three, one cell lives about an hour: updraughts build the cumulus, the mature stage starts when rain reaches the ground and is the most dangerous, and the downdraughts then choke it. The hazards reach far outside the cloud: hail under the anvil, and a gust front that can run 15 to 20 nm ahead, past the 10 nm stand-off. Stage lengths follow the lessons; the rest of the timing and the distances are illustrative.

Try it: the storm has not reached the aerodrome 15 nm away. Is it safe to land there now?

Play from Mature: the gust front spreads out from under the cell and reaches the aerodrome while the storm itself is still 15 nm away, as a squall and a wind shift. Land well before it arrives, or go elsewhere.

Try it: which looks more dangerous, a fresh rain shaft or the big anvil of an old storm?

Step to Rain at the ground, then to Dissipating: the mature stage begins when rain reaches the ground and is the most dangerous, even before the anvil has spread. The old anvil drifting off a dissipating cell looks dramatic, but its updraughts have gone (keep clear of it anyway).
