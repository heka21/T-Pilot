What to notice. Each option is time from distance and groundspeed, fuel from time and flow, then the 30-minute fixed reserve and any holding stacked on top. A bar that crosses the fuel-on-board line is struck out, because flying it means landing on the reserve. The wind can make the longer way home the cheaper one, and holding at one aerodrome can rule it out altogether.

Try it: 100 nm out with 55 L. Why is going home cheaper than continuing, when home is 20 nm further?

Home is 100 nm at 125 kt, 48 min and 29 L; on is 80 nm at 85 kt, 56 min and 34 L. The 40 kt higher groundspeed home more than pays for the extra distance. Add 30 minutes of holding at the destination and continuing needs 70 L: struck out.

Try it: in the Kalgoorlie case, how much fuel would you need on board for continuing to come back into play?

Continuing needs 32 L to get there, 32 L of holding and the 16 L reserve: 80 L. With 68 L you are 12 L short; slide the fuel up to 80 L and the bar just reaches the line.
