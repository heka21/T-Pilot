What to notice. Nothing in the cabin tells you: no smell, no breathlessness, and the first symptoms feel like an ordinary long flight. Heat off and vents open clears the cabin air in minutes, but the haemoglobin lets go of carbon monoxide only over many hours, so act on suspicion and the earlier the better. The shapes and timings here are illustrative, not measurements; the order of the symptoms and the fifteen-minute alarm are the lesson's.

Try it: the detector chirps but you feel fine and smell nothing. Act now, or wait to be sure?

Press Alarm: when the detector chirps only a few seats are taken and you feel fine. Compare Spot card and No detector: each minute of waiting takes more seats, and the judgement you would use to decide is the first thing to go. Heat off and vents open costs only some warmth.

Try it: why is the afternoon leg off even after the cabin air is clean?

Press Hours later: there is no carbon monoxide left in the cabin, but the red seats empty only slowly. Breathing ordinary air, CO takes many hours to clear, so you are still affected on the afternoon leg.
