Hazards in season

What to notice. Where the highs sit decides Perth's weather. In summer the high is to the south, Perth is on its northern side and the anticlockwise flow gives a hot, dry easterly; in winter the highs move north over the continent, Perth lies in the westerlies and cold fronts arrive every few days. The hazards follow the same calendar: storms with heating in summer and with fronts in autumn and late spring, fog in the long winter nights. The positions on the map are schematic, and the high is drawn wider than tall, as the highs often are along their belt.

Try it: a high is centred south of Esperance. What wind would you expect over Perth, and why?

Easterly. Press High south of Esperance: Perth is on the high's northern side, and round a Southern Hemisphere high the air circulates anticlockwise, so there it flows from east to west.

Try it: you are planning an early-morning flight to Northam in June. Which hazard is in season that would not be in January?

Fog. Move to June: fog season in the south-west is May to September, when the long, clear, still winter nights let the ground cool the air to its dew point, and it pools in valleys like the Avon around Northam.
