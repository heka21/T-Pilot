What to notice. Drag the point. Left of VA the stall curve sits below the limit, so however hard you pull the wing stalls first; right of VA a pull or a gust reaches the limit load before the stall. Lighter, the stall curve moves left and VA comes down with it (VA ∝ √weight), and each gust line steepens. Gust loads grow in proportion to airspeed, which is why the yellow arc is for smooth air only. The 52 kt stall speed and the limits are the lessons'; VNO, VNE, the negative stall curve and the gust line are schematic.

Try it: a normal category aeroplane stalls at 52 kt. Where is VA, and what does a full pull do at 80 kt and at 120 kt?

VA = 52 × √3.8 ≈ 101 kt. At 80 kt the wing stalls at about 2.4 g ((80 ÷ 52)²), well short of the limit. At 120 kt it could make 5.3 g before stalling, so a full pull goes past +3.8 g: Pull at 120 kt shows the point in the red.

Try it: you are solo at 80% of maximum weight. Is 101 kt still a safe speed for a full control input?

No. Press Solo: the stall speed falls to about 47 kt and VA to about 90 kt (101 × √0.8 = 101 × 0.894 ≈ 90). At 101 kt the wing can now make about 4.7 g before it stalls, past the 3.8 g limit.
