What to notice. Every avoiding turn in the picture is to the right, and whoever gives way passes behind or beside the other, never across its nose. Converging, the aircraft with the other on its right gives way; head-on, both turn right; the overtaker keeps clear. The one with right of way holds heading and speed but keeps watching. Speeds, distances and turn sizes here are illustrative.

Try it: you see another aeroplane at your 10 o’clock, at your level and closing. Who gives way?

It does: 10 o’clock is on your left, so you are on its right, the roundabout traffic with priority. You hold heading and speed and keep watching, ready to avoid it if its pilot has not seen you. Choose “On your left” to watch it turn right and pass behind you.

Try it: in the head-on case, what would happen if the other pilot turned left instead?

Both aeroplanes would turn towards the same side of the sky and could turn straight into each other. The rule works only because everyone turns right, so each passes down the other’s left side.
