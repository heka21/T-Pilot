What to notice. An ADIZ asks one question of every aircraft: is the one we see the one we were told about? Inside the track and time tolerances of your flight notification you are matched; outside them, unreported, you may be treated as unidentified and intercepted. The cure is always the same: tell ATS about any deviation as soon as possible, before the boundary. The ADIZ sits on top of the airspace class, so the Class G rules still apply. The windows are schematic: the tolerance figures, the notification lead time and the boundaries are in AIP ENR 2.2.

Try it: a cloud bank forces you well off your planned track just before the boundary. What do you do?

Choose Cloud: off track, then tick the box: tell ATS as soon as possible. An unreported deviation looks exactly like an aircraft that is not the one in the plan.

Try it: a headwind will make you reach the entry point well after your notified time. What is the risk, and the fix?

Choose Headwind: late. Outside the time tolerance you may be treated as unidentified, and an unidentified aircraft may be intercepted. Call ATS before the boundary with your revised entry time.
