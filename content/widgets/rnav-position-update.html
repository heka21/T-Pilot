Last update

What to notice. Like DR, a self-contained system's position drifts with time, and any RNAV position is only as good as its last sensor input, so it must be updated. An update shows as a jump in the displayed position and sudden changes in track, groundspeed and wind, with a sensor annunciation. Inhibit updating when the sensor is suspect: a bad update swaps a good position for a wrong one. The drift rate and timings are illustrative; the 3 nm jump is the lesson's example.

Try it: why might a pilot inhibit GNSS updating during a NOTAM'd jamming exercise?

A jammed or spoofed GNSS could feed a wrong position, and an automatic update would replace a good position with it. Inhibiting keeps the last good position, which drifts only slowly. Compare Jammed: bad update with Inhibit in the jamming.

Try it: the display jumps 3 nm. Is that always a fault?

No: a system that had drifted jumps back to the truth when it is corrected (Drift, then update). Judge every jump against an independent source, such as a visual fix.
