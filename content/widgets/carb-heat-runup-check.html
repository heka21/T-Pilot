Verdict

What to notice. Full heat should always cost RPM, typically about 100 to 150 in a trainer (your POH may give its own figure), because hot air is less dense: the drop proves hot air is reaching the carburettor. A rise while the heat is still on means there was ice, and back on cold the RPM ends higher than it started. No drop at all means the heat is not working. The RPMs are the lesson's worked example and its two variations; the timings are illustrative.

Try it: run-up at 1,700. Heat on gives 1,560, then 1,610 with the heat still on; on cold it settles at 1,740. What happened?

The drop of 1,700 − 1,560 = 140 RPM shows the heat works. The rise of 1,610 − 1,560 = 50 RPM with the heat still on means ice was present and has melted. The 1,740 on cold is 40 higher than the start, because the starting figure was already reduced by the ice. Expect more ice and use heat as the POH requires, then select cold for take-off.

Try it: you pull the knob and the RPM stays at 1,700. Can you take off?

Choose No drop: no drop means no hot air is reaching the carburettor (a disconnected control, or a damaged duct or flap). The aeroplane is unserviceable for any flight where carburettor ice is possible, and on a humid morning that is every flight.
