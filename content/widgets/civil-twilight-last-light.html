What to notice. Last light is not sunset: it is the end of civil twilight, when the sun's centre is 6° below the horizon, and the graph assumes a clear sky and a flat horizon at sea level. Hills to the west, cloud, haze or smoke take the useful light away sooner, so treat last light as a deadline with a margin; the 10-minute rule comes off last light. The 1831 last light is the lesson's Perth example; the sunset time, the rate the sun sinks and the light levels are an illustrative model.

Try it: last light at Perth is 1831 WST. When must a day VFR flight be on the ground, and is that 10 minutes before sunset?

1821 WST, 10 minutes before the end of daylight. Play the evening: the sun has already set by then; the margin comes off last light, not off sunset.

Try it: tick the hills. Why can the strip be in shadow while the graph still says daylight?

The graph assumes a flat horizon. A ridge to the west hides the sun before it reaches the true horizon and the valley fills with shadow, so useful light ends before the published last light.
