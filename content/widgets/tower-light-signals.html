What to notice. The same light means something different in the air and on the ground, but the pattern holds: green permits, red prohibits, white sends you back, and flashes are the weaker version of the steady light (green flashes: come back and land later, or taxi; steady green: land, or take off). You reply by rocking the wings in flight or moving the ailerons or rudder on the ground by day, and by flashing your lights at night. The lamp’s flash rate here is illustrative.

Try it: on final you get a steady red. What does it mean, and what will clear you to land?

Give way to other aircraft and continue circling: go around, stay in the circuit and watch the tower. A steady green clears you to land. On the ground the same steady red would mean stop.

Try it: you are taxiing and the tower shows you white flashes. Where do you go?

Back to your starting point on the aerodrome. White means “go back”; in flight the same white flashes mean land at this aerodrome and proceed to the apron, with the steady green (land) and green flashes (taxi) still to come.
