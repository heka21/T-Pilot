What to notice. Best performance comes from a moderate level of arousal, not the most. Too little and vigilance drifts; too much and attention narrows until the jobs that keep you alive, the lookout, the fuel, even flying the aeroplane, are dropped. For a complex task the peak sits at lower arousal. The curves' shapes and the order the jobs are dropped are illustrative.

Try it: the engine runs rough over the wheatbelt. Why do pilots in this state fly serviceable aeroplanes into the ground?

Press Sudden fault: arousal jumps up the right-hand side, attention narrows onto the engine and the other jobs go dark. Flying the aeroplane first keeps it lit while you deal with the problem.

Try it: the same practice forced landing, half asleep, with the examiner watching, and terrified. Which goes best?

Compare Half asleep, PFL, examiner and Terrified: only the moderate level reaches the top of the curve. Half asleep you miss the best paddock; terrified you fumble the checks.
