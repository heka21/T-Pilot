Lowest usable level

What to notice. The transition altitude is fixed at 10,000 ft on QNH, but a flight level is a pressure level: it sinks about 30 ft for every hectopascal the QNH is below 1013. So on a low-QNH day FL110 creeps down towards 10,000 ft and the layer between them thins, which is why the AIP raises the lowest usable level when the QNH is below 1013: FL115, FL120 or FL125, the first level that still sits at least 1,000 ft above 10,000 ft (the QNH bands are in the AIP ENR 1.7 table; verify the figures there). Climbing, change to 1013 at 10,000 ft; descending, change to QNH at FL110; never cruise in between.

Try it: the area QNH is 1003. How high is FL110, and how thick is the layer?

Choose QNH 1003: $11{,}000 - (1013 - 1003) \times 30 = 10{,}700$ ft, so the layer is only 700 ft thick instead of 1,000 ft. That shrinking gap is what the AIP table guards against: FL115 (about 11,200 ft today) is the lowest usable level.

Try it: descending from FL120 on a 1013 day, when do you change the subscale?

Untick Climbing and slide down: the setting stays 1013 until you pass FL110, then becomes the area or local QNH. Climbing, the change comes earlier in the picture, at 10,000 ft.
