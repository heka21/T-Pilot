The faint light

What to notice. Two curves: the cones finish adapting in about 7 to 10 minutes, the far more sensitive rods take about 30, and one bright light starts the whole climb again. Because the fovea has no rods, a faint light you stare at disappears; look 10 to 15 degrees to one side. The times are the lesson's; the sensitivity scale and the curves' shapes are illustrative.

Try it: you are 25 minutes into adapting when someone shines a torch at you. How long until you are fully adapted again, and how could you have avoided it?

Tick the torch and play on: adaptation starts from the beginning and takes about another 30 minutes. Tick Close one eye: that eye keeps its adaptation, and opening it gives you the night back at once.

Try it: after 35 minutes in the dark, why does a faint farmhouse light vanish when you look straight at it?

Press Stare at it: its image falls on the fovea, which has only cones, and the cones cannot see that faint a light. Tick Look to one side: the image moves onto the rods and the light reappears.
