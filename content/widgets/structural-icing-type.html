Effect on the aeroplane

What to notice. Structural ice needs two things together: visible moisture and an airframe at or below 0 °C. The drop size decides the type: large drops run back and freeze into hard, heavy clear ice (typically 0 to −10 °C in Cu, Cb and freezing rain); small drops freeze on impact into white rime on the leading edge (about −10 to −20 °C in layer cloud). Ice shapes and build-up speed are illustrative.

Try it: why is clear ice more dangerous than rime?

Press Clear ice in Cu and watch the shape: the water runs back before it freezes, so the ice coats the wing well aft of the leading edge in a hard, heavy sheet that is hard to see and changes the wing's shape. Rime stays on the leading edge and is white and brittle.

Try it: a clear, humid morning at 18 °C. Can ice form anywhere on the aeroplane?

Not structural ice: untick visible moisture or set the temperature above 0 and nothing builds. But carburettor ice can, inside the engine, from about −10 to +30 °C in humid air, worst at low power.
