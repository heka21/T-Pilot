What to notice. The receiver tells you that you have passed a waypoint in four ways: a waypoint alert on approach, the distance counting down to zero, TO flipping to FROM, and automatic sequencing to the next leg, early with turn anticipation. In OBS mode it never sequences, so the flag and the distance are your only cues and it will hold you on the old course. The cross-track needle shows nautical miles, not degrees; the 1 nm full scale here is one selectable setting, and en route scales are wider. Distances, the alert point and the turn are illustrative.

Try it: you left the receiver in OBS mode after a hold. What do you see as you pass the waypoint?

The flag flips from TO to FROM and the distance starts counting up, but the desired track stays on the old leg: no sequencing. Press OBS mode, then switch back to Auto-sequence.

Try it: why does the receiver change legs before you reach the waypoint?

Turn anticipation: it starts the turn early so you roll out on the next leg instead of overshooting. Compare Turn anticipation with Overfly.
