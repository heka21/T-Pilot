What to notice. A spinning rotor has two properties. Rigidity in space: roll or pitch the aeroplane and the gimbals turn so the spin axis holds its direction, which is what the attitude indicator and the directional indicator rely on. Precession: push on the rim and the rotor tilts not where you pushed but 90° further round in the direction of rotation; reverse the spin and the response moves to the other side. That is what the turn coordinator uses. The DI holds a direction in space, so friction and the Earth's rotation make it drift: reset it to the compass about every 10 to 15 minutes, in straight, level, unaccelerated flight. The drift rate drawn is illustrative; the lesson gives none.

Try this: push at the front with the rotor spinning clockwise from above. Which side dips, and what happens if you reverse the spin?

The right side dips: the force acts 90° on in the direction of rotation. Reverse the spin and the same push makes the left side dip. The response always follows the rotation, never the push.

Try this: set 15 minutes and roll the aeroplane 30°. Why must the DI be reset straight and level rather than now?

Only in straight, level, unaccelerated flight is the compass free of its turning and acceleration errors. Reset the DI from a compass that is swinging in a turn and you copy the compass's error into the instrument that would otherwise have been the more accurate of the two.
