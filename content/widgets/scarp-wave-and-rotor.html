Above the Scarp crest

What to notice. The rough air is on the lee side, which in an easterly is the Perth side of the Scarp. A moderate wind tumbles into rotor at the foot of the hills; a strong wind across the ridge in stable air sets up standing waves, with smooth lift and sink aloft, lenticulars at the crests and severe rotor beneath them at about ridge height. In dry air the waves can be there with no cloud at all. Wave spacing, heights and the lighter-wind thresholds are illustrative; the 20 kt, 30° and 2,000 ft figures are the lesson's.

Try it: a summer dawn, an 18 kt easterly at Jandakot. Where is the climb-out rough?

Press 18 kt dawn: the easterly pours down the Scarp and rolls into rotor at its foot, so below about 2,000 ft within a few miles of the hills the ride is bumpy. Climb above 2,000 ft or fly later, when the easterly eases.

Try it: on the wave day, what changes if the air turns unstable, or the wind swings along the ridge?

Press Unstable, then Along the ridge: the lenticulars and wave go in both cases, because the wave needs stable air pushed square across the ridge. Low in the lee it is still rough in the unstable air, and still bumpy with the wind along the ridge.
