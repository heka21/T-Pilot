What to notice. On one magneto each cylinder has one spark instead of two, so a healthy engine always loses a little RPM. The check asks three things: is each drop within the limit, is the difference within its limit, and was there a drop at all? No drop means the other magneto is not being earthed, and the dead cut at OFF finds the same fault. The limits are the worked example's POH figures; use your own POH's.

Try it: BOTH 1,800, left alone 1,710, right alone 1,650. Does it pass?

Press Worked example: left drop 90 (within 125), right drop 150 (over 125), difference 60 (over 50). The right side has a problem, probably a fouled plug: clear it as the POH directs (higher RPM, leaned, briefly) and repeat; if it still fails, the aeroplane is unserviceable.

Try it: you select L and the RPM does not change at all. Good news?

No. Press No drop on L: the right magneto should have been earthed but is still firing, so its P-lead or the switch is faulty. Then select OFF: the engine keeps running. With the switch at OFF that magneto is live, and moving the propeller by hand can start the engine.
