What to notice. The VOR's error is an angle, so its sideways cost grows in proportion to the distance from the station, by the 1 in 60 rule: about 0.8 nm for 1° and 4.2 nm for 5° at 50 nm, under a mile for 2° at 20 nm. VOR/DME RNAV is most accurate near the station and fails altogether outside rated coverage or behind terrain. GNSS accuracy does not depend on where the ground stations are.

Try it: with a ±2° VOR error, how far across the radial could a waypoint 20 nm out be in error?

2 × 20 ÷ 60 ≈ 0.7 nm either side. Press 20 nm, 2°, then drag the distance to 50: the same 2° now costs about 1.7 nm.

Try it: why does a waypoint 60 nm out carry a bigger error than one 15 nm out, with the same equipment?

Four times the distance, four times the sideways error: at 2°, 60 nm gives 2.0 nm and 15 nm gives 0.5 nm. And below 5,000 ft, 60 nm is right at the edge of rated coverage.
