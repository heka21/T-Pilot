What to notice. The blue share is always the same 21 percent of the column; what shrinks with height is the column, so the oxygen pressure falls with it: 177 hPa at 5,000 ft, 147 at 10,000, 105 (half) at 18,000. Only two things put it back: more total pressure (descend, or pressurise) or a bigger oxygen fraction. Pressures are the lesson's ISA figures, smoothed between them; water vapour and carbon dioxide in the lungs are left out, as in the lesson's round numbers.

Try it: what is the oxygen partial pressure at 5,000 ft, as a share of sea level?

Press 5,000: $0.21 \times 843 = 177$ hPa, and $177 \div 213 = 0.83$. Each breath pushes oxygen into the blood with about 83 percent of the sea-level pressure.

Try it: at 18,000 ft, what oxygen percentage gives you sea-level oxygen pressure back?

Press 18,000: 21 percent of 500 hPa is only 105 hPa. Slide the oxygen up, or press 18,000 + O₂: $213 \div 500 \approx 0.43$, so about 43 percent oxygen gives about 213 hPa again, ignoring water vapour.
