What to notice. B and C are ten figures each: year, month, day, then the time in UTC. Convert the whole date-time group, not just the hours: adding 8 hours to anything from 1600 UTC onwards takes you past midnight, so the Perth date is the next day, and an early-morning WST start is printed with the previous day's date. All three NOTAMs are the lesson's invented examples.

Try it: the danger area reads B 2612022130. A friend plans a sunset flight through it at 2200 WST on 2 December. Is it active?

Choose Danger area: 2130 UTC on the 2nd plus 8 hours is 0530 WST on the 3rd, so at 2200 WST on the 2nd it has not started yet. It is active from 0530 to 1300 WST on 3 December, a morning activity.

Try it: on the Geraldton NOTAM, what is the earliest WST arrival on the 15th that finds the runway open?

C is 0930 UTC on the 15th, which is 1730 WST: slide your arrival past 1730 and the marker turns green. A 1600 arrival meets a closed runway.
