What to notice. Controlled airspace is a stack of volumes. Perth's Class C starts at the surface in the control zone, then its lower limit steps up with distance (C LL 1500, 2500, 4500), with Class E (LL 8500) further out; under each step you are in Class G and need no clearance. Jandakot's Class D zone sits from the surface under the first step while the tower is open and reverts to Class G, a CTAF aerodrome, outside its ERSA hours. The restricted area needs a clearance only while it is active. The ring sizes, the D zone's upper limit and the R area are schematic (heights are exaggerated): the real limits are on the VTC and in ERSA, never on the WAC.

Try this: set 3,000 ft and watch the readout as the track leaves Jandakot. Where does it turn red, and what has happened?

Under the C LL 2500 step, 3,000 ft is inside Class C: an infringement without a clearance, even though you crossed no line on the ground. The boundary you must watch is above you. Descend below 2,500 ft, or wait until you are under the C LL 4500 step where 3,000 ft is Class G again.

Try this: untick the tower and look at Jandakot. Which three sources told you the zone's status?

The chart (VTC) for where the zone is, ERSA for the tower's hours, and NOTAMs for any change today. Outside the hours the zone is Class G and you use CTAF procedures; no clearance and no controlled-aerodrome endorsement is needed.
