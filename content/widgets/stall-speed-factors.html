What to notice. Every factor works through one of two bars: the lift the wing must make (weight, load factor from a turn or a gust, the share of weight carried by thrust) or the lift it can make at the critical angle (flap, frost, slipstream). The stall IAS moves with the square root of their ratio. Altitude moves neither: the true airspeed at the stall rises, but the ASI reads the same. Weight and bank use the lesson's formulas; the amounts for flap, power, frost and gusts are illustrative, only their directions are the lesson's.

Try it: the book says 55 kt at 1,100 kg. What is the stall speed solo at 900 kg, and in a 45° turn at full weight?

Press 900 kg: 55 × √(900 ÷ 1,100) = 55 × 0.905 ≈ 50 kt. Press 45° turn: n = 1.41, √1.41 = 1.19, 55 × 1.19 ≈ 65 kt.

Try it: which of the seven factors leaves the indicated stall speed alone?

Altitude. Press 8,000 ft: the stall IAS stays at 55 kt while the stall TAS rises to about 62 kt, because the ASI measures the same dynamic pressure the wing feels.
