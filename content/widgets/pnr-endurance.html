What to notice. The PNR is set by fuel: time out plus time back equals the safe endurance, which is the fuel after taxi and the fixed reserve (and any holding you keep) divided by the flow. More fuel moves it out; wind from either direction brings it in, and the graph is symmetrical, so a tailwind outbound only gets you there sooner. $\text{PNR} = E \times GS_\text{out} \times GS_\text{home} \div (GS_\text{out} + GS_\text{home})$.

Try it: on the 2.5 h example, swap the 10 kt headwind for a 10 kt tailwind. What changes and what does not?

The PNR stays at 136.4 nm, because the two groundspeeds appear symmetrically. What changes is the time: at 120 kt out you reach it after 1.14 h, about 68 min, instead of 82 min.

Try it: on the Kalgoorlie plan, add 30 L of fuel. Which point moves, the PNR or the ETP?

Only the PNR: 161 L available after taxi and reserve gives E = 5.03 h and a PNR about 262 nm out. The ETP (181 nm) has no fuel in it and stays put.
