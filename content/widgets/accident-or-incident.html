What to notice. The category comes from the outcome, never from how dramatic it felt. Three tests make an accident (a person killed or seriously injured, serious damage or structural failure, the aircraft missing), and only inside the window from boarding to fly to everyone disembarking. Damage limited to the engine, propeller, wheels, tyres, brakes, fairings or small dents does not count by itself, so a prop strike is an incident. If an accident nearly occurred it is a serious incident. The category then sets the reporting: accidents and serious incidents phone the ATSB on 1800 011 034 straight away and write within 72 hours; other incidents just write within 72 hours. The timeline's spacing is a picture of the 72 hours, not a schedule.

Try it: the York hard landing cracked the spar and hurt the instructor. Which test decides it, and what if only the spar had cracked?

Press York: the person test is met first (a serious injury), so it is an accident. Set People to “Nobody hurt”: the airframe test still catches it, because a cracked spar is structural failure. Either way the student or the operator phones 1800 011 034, then writes within 72 hours, and the wreckage stays where it is.

Try it: the Merredin prop strike bent the propeller tips. Why is it not an accident, and what changes if the wing is damaged too?

Press Merredin prop: damage limited to the propeller does not by itself make an accident, nobody was hurt, so it is an incident: a written report to the ATSB within 72 hours and a defect endorsement on the maintenance release. Change the damage to “Serious damage or structural failure” and it becomes an accident: phone first.
