What to notice. In level flight the relative airflow is horizontal, so each airspeed needs exactly one angle of attack, and the nose attitude is that angle less the small fixed rigging angle: fast, nose low; slow, nose high. The angles belong to the wing: about 4° gives the best glide and about 16° the stall at any weight, but a lighter aeroplane reaches them at lower speeds (70 kt becomes about 63 kt at 810 kg). Flap gives the same lift at a smaller angle, so the nose sits lower. The link holds only in level flight. The lift curve and the 1.5° rigging angle are illustrative, and so are the stall speeds that fall out of them (about 44 kt clean at 1,000 kg here, lower than the 50 to 55 kt book figures RBKA 3.6 uses): take the 4° and 16° angles and the square-root weight rule from this widget, and the stall speeds from the lesson.

Try it: level at 90 kt and then at 110 kt, same weight. Which needs the lower nose?

Press 90 kt, then 110 kt: the angle of attack falls from about 1.6° to about 0.4°, so the nose comes down by more than a degree. At the higher speed the wing makes its lift at a smaller angle.
