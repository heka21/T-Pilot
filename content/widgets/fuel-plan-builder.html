What to notice. Every line answers a different "what if", so nothing is counted twice: holding is fuel you may spend waiting out a spell (30 minutes for an INTER, 60 for a TEMPO), the alternate is fuel for when the weather never improves, and the 30-minute fixed reserve, worked at the holding flow, sits on top of both and is never spent. Keep the ledger unrounded and in one unit, then convert to kilograms at 0.72 for the loading sheet.

Try it: in the INTER plan with 110 L, change the INTER to a TEMPO. What does that do, and what are your options?

Holding doubles from 18 L to 36 L, so the fuel required goes from 99 L to 117 L: 7 L more than the tanks hold. Plan an alternate within range instead of holding, a fuel stop, or an arrival outside the TEMPO.

Try it: on the Cunderdin–Northam afternoon, add the 10% contingency. Does 130 L still give a sensible margin?

The contingency is 4.9 L (10% of the 49.1 L trip), so the minimum becomes 87.0 L. With 130 L on board that leaves about 43 L spare; the margin over the bare minimum of 82.1 L is 47.9 L.
