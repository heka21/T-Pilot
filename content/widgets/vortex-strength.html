What to notice. The vortices come from the wing working to carry the weight: heavier means more lift, slower means a higher angle of attack, and a clean wing rolls the spill-over into two tight cores. So the worst wake is heavy, clean and slow, typically just after rotation; flaps break it up and speed weakens it. Seen from behind, the left vortex turns clockwise and the right anticlockwise, with downwash between them. The strength scale and the flap effect are illustrative.

Try it: one airliner lands with full flap at its landing weight, another departs clean at its maximum take-off weight. Which wake is worse?

Press Landing, full flap (about 56% of the worst), then Departing at MTOW (about 83%): the departing aircraft is heavier and, once the flaps come up, clean and still slow, so its wake is stronger.

Try it: is a fast aircraft's wake the dangerous one?

No. Press Fast cruise: at three times its lift-off speed the wing needs a small angle of attack and the strength falls to about a third. "Fast" is the exam's distractor; heavy, clean and slow is the answer.
