What to notice. Read the position before the shape: symbols in the signal area beside the windsock report what you may use or what activity to expect (the dumbbell, two crosses for gliding, the T, the right-hand arrow), while a single cross on a runway or taxiway closes it. The ground signal of the day overrides your plan from ERSA, so a dumbbell takes away the grass you may normally use. The dumbbell-with-bars meaning follows the lesson, which flags that older material has it the other way round (verify against the current AIP AD 1.1). Layout illustrative.

Try it: ERSA says operations are not restricted to runways and the windsock favours the gravel 30. From overhead you see a white dumbbell. Where do you land?

Choose Dumbbell: only on the sealed runway. The gravel and the grass are out, usually because they are soft after rain, so it is 03/21, whatever the wind favours, if the crosswind is within your limits.

Try it: two white crosses in the signal area, or one cross at each end of a runway. Same message?

No. Two crosses beside the windsock mean gliding operations in progress; a cross at each end of a runway closes that runway. Compare Double cross and Runway crosses.
