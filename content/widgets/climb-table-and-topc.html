What to notice. Every row runs from sea level, so a climb between two altitudes is the difference between their rows, interpolated when you fall between rows. Then correct for temperature (here the lesson's 10 percent per 10 degrees above ISA, applied to time, fuel and distance), and only then for wind: the wind moves the top of climb along your track but never changes the time or the fuel. The rows are the lesson's worked examples merged into one illustrative table; your POH governs.

Try it: climb from 2,000 to 6,000 ft, 20 degrees above ISA, into a 15 kt headwind at 80 kt TAS.

ISA figures 11 − 3 = 8 min, 6 − 2 = 4 L, 15 − 4 = 11 nm. Add 20 percent: 9.6 min, 4.8 L, about 13 nm still air. Groundspeed 65 kt: 9.6 ÷ 60 × 65 = 10.4 nm over the ground.

Try it: why does a headwind not make the climb take longer or burn more fuel?

The aeroplane climbs through the moving air at the same rate, so the time to gain the height and the fuel for it are unchanged. Only the ground covered in that time changes: drag the wind and watch the TOPC slide while the time and fuel stay put.
