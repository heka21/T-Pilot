What the engine is telling you

What to notice. The carburettor meters fuel by the volume of air, so with the knob left alone the mixture follows the density: thin air, rich mixture. Climbing, it gets richer (lean in the cruise); descending, it gets leaner (enrich in steps, full rich for the pre-landing checks). Never leaning wastes fuel and fouls plugs; forgetting to enrich gives a rough, lean engine by 3,500 ft. The densities are ISA; the gauge positions, the knob travel and the timings are illustrative.

Try it: you climb from Jandakot to 6,500 ft without touching the mixture. Too rich or too lean, and what does the engine tell you?

Choose Never leaned: too rich. The air is about a fifth thinner (82% of sea-level density) but the fuel metered is the same, so the ratio is well rich: the RPM is lower than the throttle should give (2350 instead of the 2400 peak), the fuel flow is high and the plugs foul. Lean by RPM to the peak and a touch richer.

Try it: an hour later you have descended to 3,500 ft without touching the mixture, and the engine is a little rough with the RPM down. What happened?

Press Down to 3,500 ft, not enriched: the mixture set for 6,500 ft now meets air about 10% denser, so it is past the peak on the lean side. Enrich until smooth, then re-lean for 3,500 ft or go full rich if you are joining the circuit.
