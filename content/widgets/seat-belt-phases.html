What to notice. Belts are required where something sudden is most likely and there is least time to react: taxiing, the take-off and the landing. In flight they go on whenever the pilot in command directs, for example in turbulence, and a lit fasten-seat-belt sign counts as a direction. There is no height rule. The pilot in command's harness is never undone. The heights and timings of this flight are illustrative.

Try it: your passenger unfastens her belt in a smooth climb, well clear of the take-off. Is that allowed?

Only if you have not directed belts on. Choose Smooth climb: the sign is out, because the rule leaves it to the pilot in command. Tick Pilot in command directs belts on and the sign lights: once you direct it, she must obey your lawful instruction. If the air turns rough, you direct belts on.

Try it: why must the seats be locked for the take-off roll, not just the belts fastened?

Choose Take-off roll. Under acceleration an unlocked seat slides back, and the pilot's hands pull the column back with it: the nose rises at low speed, the recipe for a stall just after lift-off.
