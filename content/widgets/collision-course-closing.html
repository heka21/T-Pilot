What to notice. On a collision course the other aircraft does not move on your windscreen: the relative bearing stays constant and it only grows, slowly while it is distant and then very fast in the last few seconds. Movement across the windscreen means it will pass ahead or behind. The speeds (90 and 110 kt) and the Warrior's span are illustrative; the geometry is exact.

Try it: which is more dangerous, an aircraft sliding across your windscreen or one sitting still at one o’clock?

Play the collision: the Warrior stays on the same spot (the faint dots show where it was) and grows only at the very end. Untick the box and play again: it drifts aft and passes behind. The one that does not move is the one that hits you.

Try it: how much bigger is the Warrior 10 seconds before impact than 45 seconds before?

Drag to 10 s: the apparent size is about 4.5 times what it was at the start, still well under a degree. Most of the growth comes in the last few seconds, which is why it seems to blossom out of nowhere.
