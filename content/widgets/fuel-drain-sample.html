do not start

What to notice. Water is heavier than fuel, so once it has settled it is the first thing out of the drain: a clear layer or globules at the bottom of the tester. Sample too soon and it is still stirred through the fuel as cloudiness. Keep draining until the samples are clean, and after water, two clean in a row. A colourless sample from an AVGAS aeroplane is water or jet fuel. How many samples it takes here is illustrative; the lesson gives no figure.

Try it: the lesson's wet-week morning. The first tester has a clear layer under the blue fuel. What now?

Press After a wet week, then Drain a sample until the verdict changes: the layer shrinks to globules, then the samples come out clean blue. You stop only after two clean samples in a row, and you report the cap seal.

Try it: the sample is clear all the way through. Is that clean fuel?

Press All water: from an AVGAS aeroplane a colourless sample is water (you have not reached the fuel yet) or jet fuel. Smell it: Jet fuel smells of paraffin, so press Jet fuel to see the other case. Either way, do not fly until it is sorted out; jet fuel means drain and flush, not just drain.
