What to notice. Aileron's further effects come in a sequence. First adverse yaw: the down-going aileron makes the rising wing produce more lift and more induced drag, so the nose swings away from the roll. Then, once banked, the aeroplane slips towards the lower wing and the fin weathercocks the nose towards it; left alone the nose drops and the bank and speed build into a spiral descent. Rudder in the same direction as the aileron keeps the nose on the path and the ball in the middle. The yaw angles, timings and speeds are illustrative.

Try it: you roll left with aileron alone at low speed. Which way does the nose swing first?

To the right, away from the roll: the right aileron goes down, so the right wing makes more lift and more induced drag and is held back. Left rudder with the left aileron cancels it. (The widget rolls right; mirror it.)

Try it: why does the nose later swing towards the lower wing?

Press Slip: with the lift tilted the aeroplane slides sideways towards the low wing, the air arrives from that side and pushes on the big side area of the fin and rear fuselage, and like a weathervane the nose swings into it.
