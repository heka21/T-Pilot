## The rule: one list, not both

You may use **either** the Airservices list **or** the Jeppesen list. You must supply these materials
yourself, and you **cannot combine** materials from both lists. Unless otherwise stated, all
regulations, orders and air publications must be **current for the date of the exam**. You cannot
borrow anything from other candidates during the exam.

If you use the Airservices AIP you use Airservices plates, maps and charts; if you use the Jeppesen
Airway Manual you use the Jeppesen plates, maps and charts.

**Note 1:** the VFRG and the CASR Part 91 plain English guide may not have current information. If you
use them, make sure you also have other materials covering current laws and procedures. The VFRG
means the document produced by **CASA**, not by another party. The guides carry CASA's notice that
they are for information only and are **not the law**. The VFRG is permitted for PPL and RPL exams only.

## Self-printed documents

Self-printed copies downloaded from the CASA website are acceptable for the AIP, CASR, CAR, CAOs,
CAAPs, the VFRG (PPL and RPL exams) and the Plain English guides, provided that:

- you only have documents permitted for your exam;
- the pages are **bound** together, with no loose sheets (any binding: ring binder, lever arch, staples);
- the documents contain **no manuscript material** and are each in a clearly marked folder with no other documents, notes or material;
- they are printed on paper **no larger than A4**, directly from the website with no alterations, including headers and footers.

Self-printed ERSA, DAPs, ERCs, WACs or other maps are **not** approved.

## Marking and tagging

Underlining and highlighting are allowed; **notations are not**.

| Publication | Tags permitted |
|---|---|
| CAR (1988) | no tags |
| CASR (1998), all Parts and the relevant MOSs | no tags |
| CAO | no tags |
| AIP Book (or its Jeppesen equivalent) | **maximum 15** |
| Visual Flight Rules Guide (VFRG) | no tags |
| ERSA | **maximum 5** |
| Handbook and Operations Manual of exam aircraft | no tags |
| All charts | no tags |
| Plain English Guides (PEGs) | no tags |
| Any other exam-permitted publication | no tags |

With too many tags you cannot sit the exam until you remove them. Tags must be in English and cannot
include notes, formulae, explanatory notes, cross-references, sketches, diagrams, paste-on materials
or training aide memoires. Airservices or Jeppesen section dividers are not counted as tags.

## Navigation equipment

"Nav equipment" means: dividers, compass, protractor, ruler (or straight-edge), and a non-electronic
wind triangle and circular slide rule computer (for example a Jeppesen CR) or a manual slide rule.
Only **one** manual pilot computer is allowed.

## The "basic calculator"

A basic calculator has **only**:

- the four functions (+, -, x, ÷);
- square root (√);
- a single memory;
- optionally a percentage key (%) and GST+ / GST- keys.

It must not be programmable or an "aviation computer": no hours:minutes:seconds (HMS) key, no
scientific functions (sin, cos, tan), no CAS/TAS/Mach conversions, and nothing that solves aviation
problems such as ground speed, heading, wind components or in-flight winds. Aspeq venues keep a stock
of calculators, and you can borrow one if yours fails.

## Provided by the invigilator

You hand all of these back at the end, including scribble paper and the workbook:

- 1 blue and 1 black ballpoint pen, 1 red fineliner
- 1 HB pencil and 1 0.5 mm mechanical pencil, 1 pencil sharpener
- 1 eraser, 1 ruler
- 2 pages of scribble paper (more on request)
- the RPL, PPL and CPL (Aeroplane) Workbook

Nothing you write on the scribble paper may leave the room.

## Not permitted

- dictionaries or English translators of any kind
- recording devices of any kind
- commercially published or home-made content pages and indexes for regulations, orders and the AIP
- manuals, textbooks, notes, blank paper or any document not on your exam's permitted list
- electronic devices: mobile phones, pagers, cameras, laptops, tablets, music players or similar
- electronic flight planning computers or devices
- anything else not on the permitted list for your exam

Using these can end your exam. Copying or removing exam material can lead to a fail and a 12-month ban
from exams.
