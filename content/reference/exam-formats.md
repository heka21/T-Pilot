## At a glance

| | RPLA | PPLA |
|---|---|---|
| Licence | Recreational pilot licence (aeroplane) | Private pilot licence (aeroplane) |
| Time allowed | 2 hours | 3.5 hours |
| Pass mark | at least 70% of the marks | at least 70% of the marks |
| Question types | multiple choice, plus questions where you type a **whole number** in a box | multiple choice, plus questions where you type a **whole number** in a box |
| Fee (each attempt) | $189.53 ($124.53 invigilator fee + $65 CASA fee) | $217.24 ($152.24 invigilator fee + $65 CASA fee) |
| Syllabus | Part 61 MOS Schedule 3 units 1.1.1 BAKC, 1.1.2 RBKA, 1.5.1 RFRC, 1.8.1 RMTC and 1.6.1 PHFC | Part 61 MOS Schedule 3 (PPL units) |
| Workbook | RPL, PPL and CPL (Aeroplane) Workbook | RPL, PPL and CPL (Aeroplane) Workbook |

CASA does not publish the number of questions. The mock exams in this app use 60 questions for both,
which is a simulation choice, not a CASA figure.

The PPLA units in this app follow the PPL units of Schedule 3: PAKC, PAKA, GNSSC, PFRA, PHFC, PNVC,
PMTC, POPC and POPA. **PHFC (human factors) appears in both exams.**

## Fuel policy

From both exam pages: fuel reserves in the RPLA and PPLA exams are carried in accordance with
**CASR Part 91 MOS, Chapter 19, table 19.02(2), Item 1**. This applies to aeroplanes with MTOW less
than 5,700 kg (piston engine or turboprop) under VFR by day.

CASA recommends that you **avoid rounding off at intermediate stages** of your workings when
calculating fuel requirements.

The workbook (v3.0a) adds that the specific gravity of AVGAS used for all loading systems is
**0.72 kg/litre**.

## Common errors listed by CASA

CASA lists these as weak areas for candidates. Spend extra time on them.

**RPLA**

- glide speeds and lift/drag ratio
- effect of wind shear on aircraft performance
- interpretation of meteorological forecasts and requirements
- local winds: sea breeze, land breeze, anabatic and katabatic winds, etc.
- use of the take-off and landing charts

**PPLA**

- correct use of the take-off and landing charts
- finding time/distance and revision of estimates
- working out density height and/or pressure height
- correct use of the various loading systems (ALPHA, BRAVO and CHARLIE)
- interpretation of meteorological forecasts
